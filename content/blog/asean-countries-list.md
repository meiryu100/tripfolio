**ASEAN has 11 member countries** as of 2026: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste and Vietnam. For decades the answer was ten. Timor-Leste became the 11th member on 26 October 2025. Below is the full ASEAN countries list with the date each country joined, how the group grew, and what the list means if you're ticking off Southeast Asia on a map.

## What is ASEAN, and which countries are in it?

ASEAN is the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, a regional group based in Jakarta, Indonesia. It was founded on 8 August 1967, when the foreign ministers of five countries signed the ASEAN Declaration in Bangkok. Those five were Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand.

Here are all 11 members, with the date each joined. The dates match Wikipedia's article on [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN) and its list of [member states of ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_ASEAN), and ASEAN's own pages give the same ones:

1. Brunei: 7 January 1984
2. Cambodia: 30 April 1999
3. Indonesia: 8 August 1967 (founder)
4. Laos: 23 July 1997
5. Malaysia: 8 August 1967 (founder)
6. Myanmar: 23 July 1997
7. Philippines: 8 August 1967 (founder)
8. Singapore: 8 August 1967 (founder)
9. Thailand: 8 August 1967 (founder)
10. Timor-Leste: 26 October 2025
11. Vietnam: 28 July 1995

The list is alphabetical, so it's easy to check against your own travels.

## How did ASEAN grow from 5 to 11 countries?

The group grew in five steps:

- **1967:** the five founders, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand.
- **1984:** Brunei.
- **1995:** Vietnam.
- **1997:** Laos and Myanmar, on the same day.
- **1999:** Cambodia, which completed the group of ten.
- **2025:** Timor-Leste, the 11th member, joined at the 47th ASEAN Summit.

Timor-Leste is the only new member in more than 25 years. The 2026 summit, hosted by the Philippines, which holds the ASEAN chair this year, was the first one it attended as a full member.

## Is Timor-Leste really an ASEAN member now?

Yes. Older lists, articles and even some travel sites still say ASEAN has 10 members, because that was true from 1999 until October 2025. If a page lists only ten countries and leaves out Timor-Leste (also called East Timor), it predates the accession.

## Which countries want to join ASEAN?

Papua New Guinea is the one country currently seeking membership, according to Wikipedia's ASEAN article, and it has held observer status since 1976. Fiji and Bangladesh are described there as seeking observer status. Observer status is not membership, so none of them count in the 11. We list Papua New Guinea under Oceania, not Asia.

## Are ASEAN countries all in Southeast Asia?

Yes, all 11 sit in Southeast Asia, and no other country does. This matters if you use a geographic list instead of a political one. In Tripfolio's country data, the "South-Eastern Asia" subregion contains exactly these 11 countries, which follows the UN geoscheme. So the ASEAN list and the Southeast Asia list are the same today.

The region splits roughly in two:

- **Mainland:** Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Thailand and Vietnam, plus the western half of Malaysia on the Malay Peninsula.
- **Maritime:** Brunei, Indonesia, the Philippines, Singapore and Timor-Leste, plus the Bornean half of Malaysia.

All 11 are in Asia on Tripfolio's map. If you want the continent-level picture, see our guide to [how many countries are in Asia](/blog/how-many-countries-in-asia).

## How big are the ASEAN countries?

They vary enormously. Using the figures in Wikipedia's member table (areas and populations come from different years, so treat them as rough):

- **Largest by area:** Indonesia, about 1.9 million km², and also the most populous, at roughly 256 million people.
- **Smallest by area:** Singapore, about 707 km², with around 5.5 million people.
- **Brunei:** about 5,765 km² and roughly 412,000 people, the smallest population in the group.
- **Timor-Leste:** about 14,900 km² and roughly 1.2 million people.
- **Philippines and Vietnam:** both near 100 million people.

If you like very small countries, Singapore and Brunei are good additions to a list; our guide to the [smallest countries in the world](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world) covers the extreme end.

## How is ASEAN different from the EU?

They are often compared, but they work differently. The EU has a shared legal system and a currency used by 21 of its 27 members. ASEAN is a looser association of governments. For counting countries, the key difference is simply membership: see our list of [countries in the European Union](/blog/countries-in-the-european-union) for the European side. Neither list tells you where you can travel freely, so check entry rules for each country before you go.

## FAQ

### How many countries are in ASEAN?

Eleven. Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste and Vietnam are all members. Timor-Leste joined on 26 October 2025.

### Who are the founding members of ASEAN?

Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand signed the ASEAN Declaration in Bangkok on 8 August 1967.

### Is Timor-Leste in ASEAN?

Yes. It became the 11th member on 26 October 2025. Lists that show only ten countries are out of date.

### Is Papua New Guinea in ASEAN?

No. It has been an observer since 1976 and is the only country currently seeking full membership, so it isn't one of the 11.

### Which ASEAN country joined first and which joined last?

Five countries were there from the start in 1967. The most recent is Timor-Leste, in 2025. Before that, Cambodia was the last to join, in 1999.
