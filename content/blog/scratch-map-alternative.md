The best scratch map alternative is a **digital travel map**: a web page or app where you tap each country you've visited and it colors in, with no foil, no shipping and no coin. It does the same job as a scratch-off poster, and it also counts your countries, covers regions inside big countries, and can be shared with one link. A poster is still better as wall art, so the right choice depends on what you want from your map.

This guide compares the two honestly, lists what to look for in a digital version, and shows how to set one up in about ten minutes.

## Why look for a scratch map alternative at all?

A scratch-off map is a lovely idea: a poster with a foil layer, and you scratch away each country you've visited. People look for something else for a few practical reasons:

- **You can't undo a scratch.** Scratched the wrong country, or counted a layover you later decide shouldn't count? The foil doesn't grow back.
- **Small places are hard to scratch.** Luxembourg, Singapore, Liechtenstein and the Caribbean islands are tiny dots on a world poster.
- **A poster doesn't keep score.** You see the colors, but you still count by hand to know your total.
- **One map, one place.** It hangs in one room. You can't check it from the airport, and you can't send it to a friend.
- **Detail is limited.** Most world posters show countries only. If you've driven through 30 US states, you'd need a separate US map.
- **It takes shipping and space.** A good poster needs a flat wall and a frame, which is not always possible in a rented flat or a dorm.

None of these makes a scratch map a bad choice. They're the reasons a second option is worth having.

## Scratch-off poster vs digital map: what's actually different?

Here is the comparison in plain language, one point at a time.

### Look and feel

A scratch map wins here. Revealing a country is satisfying, the poster looks good on a wall, and it makes a thoughtful gift. A digital map can't match the physical ritual, although a good one gives you a clean colored world to screenshot and post.

### Fixing mistakes and changing your mind

Digital wins. You can tap a country again to remove it. That matters more than it sounds, because there is no official rule for what counts as visiting a country. If you change your mind about airport layovers, you can change your map without buying a new poster. Our guide to [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) walks through the common rules.

### Counting

Digital wins. A poster leaves the math to you, and the math is less obvious than it looks. The UN has [193 member states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states) and [two non-member observer states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/non-member-states), the Holy See and the State of Palestine. That gives the 195 that many travelers and Tripfolio count as sovereign countries. A digital map keeps that tally for you, along with your percentage of the world. If the count itself confuses you, read [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world).

### Detail inside countries

Digital usually wins. Many posters stop at country borders. Digital maps can split big countries into regions, so a road trip across the American West shows up on the map and not just as one color for "USA".

### Sharing

Digital wins. A link or a picture of your map reaches friends and family in seconds. A poster on your wall is seen by whoever visits your home.

### Cost and effort

A poster is a purchase, a delivery and a frame. A digital map is free in many cases and ready as soon as the page loads. Prices of posters vary a lot by size and finish, so check the current price of any specific product before you decide.

### Longevity

A good poster can last for years on a wall, and it looks better as it fills in. A digital map lasts as long as the service does, so it helps to pick one that lets you share or save a picture of your map, in case you ever want to keep a copy.

## What should you look for in a digital travel map?

Not every app that colors in countries is equally useful. Before you pick one, check these things:

1. **Does it work where you are?** A web-based map opens on any phone or laptop with nothing to install. A store app only works on the platform it was built for.
2. **Can you undo?** The whole point is flexibility. Tap on, tap off.
3. **Does it count properly?** Look for a clear total, ideally one that says which list it uses and how territories are treated.
4. **Does it handle regions?** If you road trip, you want US states, and perhaps Canadian provinces or Australian states too.
5. **Is there a wishlist?** Half the fun of a map is the places still blank. A second color for "want to go" turns the map into a plan.
6. **Can you control who sees it?** A map of everywhere you've been says a lot about you. Look for privacy settings you understand.
7. **Is it free, and is there an ad wall?** Check before you spend an evening entering countries.

## How Tripfolio works as a scratch map alternative

[Tripfolio](/countries-visited-map) is built around exactly this: a world map where you color the countries you've visited and the ones you want to visit. It's free and runs in the browser, so it works on your phone with nothing to install.

Here is what that looks like in practice:

- **Visited and wishlist colors.** Visited countries turn green and the ones you want to see turn orange, so a glance shows your past and your plans.
- **A real count.** Only the 195 sovereign countries count toward your "x / 195" total, your percentage and your milestones at 1, 10, 25, 50 and 100. Territories are colored on the map but kept out of the count, so the number stays honest.

- **Trips and photos.** If you want more than colors, you can attach a trip with dates, cities, a short story and photos to each country.

You don't need an account to try it. The map on the countries visited page lets you tap countries and build a share card first, and you can sign up afterwards if you want to keep it.

## Can you use both a poster and a digital map?

Yes, and plenty of travelers do. A reasonable setup:

- Keep the **digital map as your record**. It does the counting, handles regions, and is the version you share.
- Hang a **poster as the decoration**. Scratch a country when you get home from a trip, as a small ritual.

If you do, update the digital map first. It's the one that's easy to correct if you reconsider what counts.

## How to set up your digital map in ten minutes

1. **Decide your rule for "visited".** Overnight stays only, or any time on the ground? Write it down so you stay consistent.
2. **List the places from memory first.** Go region by region: Europe, Asia, the Americas, Africa, Oceania.
3. **Check your photo library.** Scrolling back through old albums often reveals a country you forgot, like a day trip across a border.
4. **Tap the countries on the map.** Start with the easy big ones, then the small ones you have to zoom in for.
5. **Add the wishlist.** Pick five places you actually want to go in the next two years. [Japan](/countries/japan) and [Portugal](/countries/portugal) are popular starting points, and the country pages give you a quick overview of each.
6. **Share it.** Send the card to a friend and ask for theirs back.

## FAQ

### What is a good alternative to a scratch-off map?

A digital travel map. You tap countries to mark them visited, fix mistakes whenever you like, and get an automatic count. Tripfolio is a free web-based option that also supports a wishlist and regional maps for the USA, Canada and Australia.

### Are scratch maps worth it?

They are worth it if you want wall art and enjoy the scratching ritual. They are less useful as a tracking tool, because they can't be undone, don't count for you, and usually show countries only.

### Can I make a scratch map for free?

You can make a free digital version by coloring in countries on an online travel map. Physical scratch-off posters are products you buy, though the price varies by brand, size and finish.

### How many countries should be on a world map?

Many travelers use 195: the 193 UN member states plus the two UN observer states, the Holy See and the State of Palestine. Tripfolio uses this list for its "x / 195" counter.

### Can I track US states on a digital travel map?

Yes. Tripfolio splits the USA into its 50 states with an "x / 50 states" counter, and there is a separate "USA only" tab on the map.
