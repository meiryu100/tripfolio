There are **46 island countries** among the UN's member states, and 48 if you also count Taiwan and Northern Cyprus, which have limited recognition. That is roughly a quarter of the world's independent countries. The exact number depends on one question: does a country have to be entirely on islands, or is sitting on part of an island enough? This guide gives the full list, grouped by ocean, and explains every edge case so you can count them on your own map.

## What is an island country?

An island country is a sovereign state whose main territory is made up of one or more islands, or parts of islands. [Wikipedia's article on island countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country) uses that wording, and it is the definition used here.

The wording matters because it lets in a few countries that share an island with a neighbor: Ireland, Haiti and the Dominican Republic, Brunei, Timor-Leste, and Papua New Guinea. It also decides the biggest argument in the field, which is Australia (more on that below).

## How many island countries are there?

[Wikipedia's list of island countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_island_countries) gives these totals:

- **46** UN member states
- **48** if you add two states with limited recognition, Taiwan and Northern Cyprus
- **50** if you also add the Cook Islands and Niue, which are in free association with New Zealand

Other sites print 47 or 51 because they draw the line differently, usually on Australia or on countries that only share an island. When you see a different number, check which of those cases it includes.

## The full list of island countries by ocean

### Caribbean Sea (12)

Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Cuba, Dominica, the Dominican Republic, Grenada, Haiti, Jamaica, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Trinidad and Tobago.

The Bahamas is also an island country. Wikipedia files it under the Atlantic Ocean, but most people group it with the Caribbean. Our [guide to the countries of the Caribbean](/blog/how-many-countries-in-the-caribbean) counts 13 sovereign states there, including the Bahamas.

### Pacific Ocean (14 UN members, 15 with Taiwan)

Fiji, Japan, Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Nauru, New Zealand, Palau, Papua New Guinea, Samoa, the Solomon Islands, Tonga, Tuvalu and Vanuatu. Taiwan makes 15 if you count states with limited recognition.

The Pacific is where island countries cluster. Thirteen of these 14 are in Oceania, and only Japan is outside it. Our [Oceania guide](/blog/how-many-countries-in-oceania) explains the region's 14 sovereign states.

### Indian Ocean (6)

Comoros, Madagascar, the Maldives, Mauritius, Seychelles and Sri Lanka. Indonesia lies across both the Indian and Pacific oceans (see the next section).

### Atlantic Ocean (6)

The Bahamas, Cape Verde, Iceland, Ireland, São Tomé and Príncipe, and the United Kingdom.

### Mediterranean Sea (2, or 3 with Northern Cyprus)

Cyprus and Malta. Northern Cyprus, which has limited recognition, occupies the same island as Cyprus.

### Persian Gulf (1)

Bahrain.

### Maritime Southeast Asia (5)

Brunei, Indonesia, the Philippines, Singapore and Timor-Leste.

Add the groups up and you get the 46. The Bahamas is counted once, in the Atlantic list, and Indonesia once, in this one.

## Which countries are on shared islands?

Nine of the 46 do not have their island to themselves. These are the ones people forget when they assume an island nation has no land border:

- **Ireland and the United Kingdom** share the island of Ireland.
- **Haiti and the Dominican Republic** share Hispaniola.
- **Brunei and Indonesia** share Borneo, together with Malaysia.
- **Indonesia and Papua New Guinea** share New Guinea.
- **Indonesia and Timor-Leste** share Timor.
- **Cyprus and Northern Cyprus** share Cyprus.

So several "island countries" do have a land border. If you are logging countries, a shared island is a natural place to add a second one on the same trip.

## Is Australia an island country?

Usually not. Australia is generally treated as a continental landmass, not an island, and Wikipedia's island rankings leave it out for that reason. It has no land borders, which is why it was sometimes called an island country, but geographers count it as part of a continent. If you want Australia on your list of islands, you can count it, but then you should say so, because most lists will not match yours.

For travelers, the useful part is the next sentence: Tripfolio files Australia in Oceania as a sovereign country, and tracks its states and territories separately. Our [Australian states guide](/blog/australian-states-visited) shows how.

## The biggest island countries

[Wikipedia's list of islands by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) ranks the world's largest islands. Here are the ones that belong to island countries, with their area:

1. **New Guinea**, 773,751 km², shared by Indonesia and Papua New Guinea
2. **Borneo**, 723,154 km², shared by Brunei, Indonesia and Malaysia
3. **Madagascar**, 592,521 km², all of it one country
4. **Sumatra**, 428,134 km², in Indonesia
5. **Honshu**, 228,296 km², the main island of Japan
6. **Great Britain**, 218,635 km², the main island of the United Kingdom

Greenland is the largest island at 2.1 million km², but it is a territory of the Danish Realm, not a sovereign country. Baffin, Victoria and Ellesmere islands are in Canada, which is not an island country.

By area and by population, **Indonesia** is the largest island country. It is also the one with the most islands, according to Wikipedia.

## Archipelagos: countries made of many islands

Some island countries are one big island with a few small ones. Others are scattered across the sea. Wikipedia lists the Bahamas, Indonesia, Japan, the Maldives, the Philippines and Seychelles among the countries spread over hundreds or thousands of smaller islands.

The shape of an island country also raises the question of what "visited" means when you only touch one island of many. We cover that in [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country).

## Island countries that sit close together

Geography groups the list into neighborhoods, which helps if you want to plan several at once:

- **Caribbean:** twelve of the 46 are in the Caribbean Sea, so one region covers a quarter of the list.
- **Pacific:** fourteen UN members, from Japan to Fiji and New Zealand.
- **North Atlantic:** [Iceland](/countries/iceland) and Ireland are both here, along with the United Kingdom.
- **Indian Ocean:** [Sri Lanka](/countries/sri-lanka) and the [Maldives](/countries/maldives) are neighbors, and Madagascar, Comoros, Mauritius and Seychelles are in the same ocean.

The smallest of the 46 are tiny. If that is your angle, see our list of the [smallest countries in the world](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world), where several island nations appear.

## FAQ

### How many island countries are there in the world?

There are 46 UN member states that are entirely or partly on islands. With Taiwan and Northern Cyprus the count is 48, and with the Cook Islands and Niue it is 50. The difference comes from how each list treats recognition and shared islands.

### What is the largest island country?

Indonesia is the largest island country by area and by population, and it has the most islands of any island country.

### Is Australia an island country?

Usually it is treated as a continental landmass, not an island country. It has no land borders, which is why it is sometimes called one, but most lists leave it out.

### Which island countries share an island with another country?

Ireland and the United Kingdom share Ireland, Haiti and the Dominican Republic share Hispaniola, and Indonesia shares Borneo, New Guinea and Timor with Brunei, Papua New Guinea and Timor-Leste. Cyprus shares its island with the limited-recognition Northern Cyprus.

### Is the United Kingdom an island country?

Yes. Wikipedia lists it as one: it covers Great Britain, part of the island of Ireland and several smaller island groups.
