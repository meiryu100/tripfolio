If you're asking "how many US states have I been to?", the answer is a number between 0 and 50, and the fastest way to get it is to go region by region instead of state by state. The United States has 50 states, and the Census Bureau groups them into four regions, so you can count the Northeast, Midwest, South and West separately and add them up.

You can also skip the counting and tap them on the [US states visited map](/us-states-visited-map), which shows your total out of 50 as you go and needs no sign-up.

This guide gives you four ways to count, a rule for the gray areas, and a way to spot which part of the country you've been ignoring.

## Why is the number so hard to remember?

States blur. You drive through three of them on a Saturday, change planes in a fourth and sleep in a fifth, and a year later you can't say which was which. Most people who try to list their states from memory come up short, then remember three more in the shower.

The fix is to stop recalling randomly and use a structure that forces you to look at every part of the map. Here are four of them.

## Way 1: Count by Census region

The Census Bureau splits the country into four regions and nine divisions (the [Census regions and divisions map](https://www2.census.gov/geo/pdfs/maps-data/maps/reference/us_regdiv.pdf) is the official reference; the lists below follow [Wikipedia's summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_of_the_United_States)). Going division by division means you never skip a corner of the map.

### Northeast: 9 states

- **New England (6):** Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, Vermont.
- **Mid-Atlantic (3):** New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania.

### Midwest: 12 states

- **East North Central (5):** Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Wisconsin.
- **West North Central (7):** Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, North Dakota, South Dakota.

### South: 16 states

- **South Atlantic (8 states):** Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia, West Virginia. The Census Bureau also places Washington, D.C. here, but D.C. is not a state.
- **East South Central (4):** Alabama, Kentucky, Mississippi, Tennessee.
- **West South Central (4):** Arkansas, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Texas.

### West: 13 states

- **Mountain (8):** Arizona, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah, Wyoming.
- **Pacific (5):** Alaska, California, Hawaii, Oregon, Washington.

Check: 9 + 12 + 16 + 13 = 50.

Walk through each division and write down how many you've visited. A tally like "New England 4/6, Mid-Atlantic 2/3, Mountain 1/8" is easy to total, and it also shows you at a glance where your gaps are.

## Way 2: Count by life chapter

Regions work for geography. Life chapters work for memory. Go through your life in order and note the states for each period:

- **Where you grew up and every place you've lived.** Usually two to four states before you've traveled anywhere.
- **School years.** Class trips, sports tournaments, college visits and the state you moved to for college.
- **Jobs and relocations.** Conferences, business trips and the year you spent somewhere "temporarily".
- **Holidays and family.** Grandparents, weddings, reunions, the one cousin who lives somewhere remote.
- **Road trips.** Cross-country drives rack up states fast, including some you only crossed.

Do this once, slowly, and you'll usually find five to ten states you'd have forgotten.

## Way 3: Count from your photos

Your camera roll is an honest witness. Scroll through it by year and write down the state for every trip you see. Sunsets in unfamiliar parking lots, a menu from a diner, a sign at a state line: they all count as evidence.

## Way 4: Count with a rule

The tricky part isn't remembering. It's deciding what counts. There is no official ruling, so choose a rule and apply it to every state the same way:

- **Overnight rule:** you slept there. The strictest and the cleanest.
- **Stopped rule:** you stopped for fuel, food, a night or a short walk. Passing through at highway speed doesn't count.
- **Foot-on-the-ground rule:** you stepped out of the car or airport and actually set foot in the state.
- **Anything rule:** if you've crossed the state line in any way, it counts, even from a train window.

A layover is the classic gray area. A common line: if you never left the terminal, it doesn't count; if you left the airport and had a meal in town, it does. Our guide to [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) walks through the same debate at the world level, and the arguments are the same.

Pick one rule and stick to it. Your number is only meaningful to you, and a consistent number is more satisfying than a flattering one.

## What isn't a state?

Some places look like states on a map but aren't one of the 50:

- **Washington, D.C.** is a federal district, not a state.
- **Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands** are U.S. territories, not states.

Tripfolio's counter works the same way: it counts the 50 states out of 50 and leaves D.C. and the territories out of that number.

## What's a good number?

There's no good or bad number. Someone who grew up in New England can pass 10 states on a single weekend drive. Someone from Texas or Alaska can spend years inside one state. Whatever number you get, the gaps are the interesting part: the one region where you're at 0/8 is probably your next road trip.

## FAQ

### How many US states have I been to?

Count by region: the Northeast has 9 states, the Midwest 12, the South 16 and the West 13, for 50 in total. Go through each region and tally the ones you've visited, or tap them on a states map that counts out of 50.

### Does a layover count as visiting a state?

There's no official rule. Most travelers say it counts only if you left the airport, for example for a meal or a night. If you stayed inside the terminal, it usually doesn't.

### Is Washington, D.C. one of the 50 states?

No. Washington, D.C. is a federal district, and Puerto Rico, Guam and the other territories aren't states either. None of them count toward a 50-state total.

### What's the fastest way to count my states?

Use the regions: go division by division and write down a tally for each, then add them up. A states map does the adding for you and shows the gaps.

### Does driving through a state count?

It depends on your rule. Many people require a stop for fuel, food or a night's sleep; others count any crossing. Choose one rule and use it for every state.
