There are **14 countries in Oceania**. That is the number of sovereign states, all of them United Nations members, in the region that stretches from Australia and New Zealand across the islands of the Pacific. On top of those 14 sit a dozen or so territories, such as Guam, French Polynesia and New Caledonia, which belong to other countries and are not counted as countries on their own.

This guide gives you the 14 countries grouped by region, the territories, the two tricky cases (the Cook Islands and Niue), and a simple way to count Oceania on your own travel map.

## How many countries are in Oceania?

Fourteen. The United Nations' [standard country and area codes](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/) file 29 entries under Oceania. Fourteen of those are sovereign states and members of the UN. The other 15 are territories and dependencies of Australia, New Zealand, France, the United Kingdom and the United States, or uninhabited outlying areas.

Oceania is the smallest of the continents by land area, and it is the second least populated after Antarctica, with roughly 44 million people according to [Wikipedia's Oceania article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania). Most of them live in Australia.

Our own list at Tripfolio has 27 entries for Oceania: the 14 countries and 13 territories. The UN adds two uninhabited or tiny areas we leave out: Heard Island and McDonald Islands, and the United States Minor Outlying Islands.

## The 14 countries of Oceania, by region

Oceania is usually split into four regions. The names come from the UN scheme and are the ones you will see on most maps.

### Australia and New Zealand (2 countries)

- **Australia**: the largest country in Oceania and the only one big enough to be called a continent on its own. See our [Australia page](/countries/australia).
- **New Zealand**: two main islands, plus a long list of Pacific links. See our [New Zealand page](/countries/new-zealand).

### Melanesia (4 countries)

- **Fiji**
- **Papua New Guinea**
- **Solomon Islands**
- **Vanuatu**

Papua New Guinea shares the island of New Guinea with Indonesia. The Indonesian half is counted as part of Indonesia (and Asia) on most lists, not as part of Oceania. Read more on the [Papua New Guinea page](/countries/papua-new-guinea) or the [Fiji page](/countries/fiji).

### Micronesia (5 countries)

- **Kiribati**
- **Marshall Islands**
- **Micronesia** (the Federated States of Micronesia)
- **Nauru**
- **Palau**

Nauru is a single island. It is also one of the smallest countries in the world, which we cover in our guide to the [smallest countries in the world](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world).

### Polynesia (3 countries)

- **Samoa**
- **Tonga**
- **Tuvalu**

That makes 2 + 4 + 5 + 3 = 14.

## Which territories are in Oceania?

These are on the UN list but are not countries. Tripfolio colors them on the map without counting them toward your total.

- **Australia and New Zealand region:** Christmas Island, Cocos (Keeling) Islands, Norfolk Island (all Australian territories)
- **Melanesia:** New Caledonia (France)
- **Micronesia:** Guam and Northern Mariana Islands (United States)
- **Polynesia:** American Samoa (United States), French Polynesia and Wallis and Futuna (France), Pitcairn Islands (United Kingdom), Tokelau (New Zealand)

Add the Cook Islands and Niue, which are in a category of their own, and you have Tripfolio's 13 Oceania territories. The UN list also includes the Heard Island and McDonald Islands and the United States Minor Outlying Islands, which almost no traveler will ever visit.

## What about the Cook Islands and Niue?

These two are the reason you sometimes see "16 countries in Oceania".

Both are self-governing states in free association with New Zealand. They run their own affairs, and the Cook Islands runs its own foreign policy and diplomatic network. According to Wikipedia's articles on the [Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands) and [Niue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niue), neither is a UN member state, and most of their people hold New Zealand citizenship. A New Zealand foreign ministry spokesperson has said the Cook Islands "is not a fully independent and sovereign state."

So are they countries? In everyday speech, many people say yes. For counting purposes, we follow the UN: sovereign means UN member, so the Cook Islands and Niue are listed as territories on Tripfolio's map. That keeps the world total at 195 (the 193 UN members plus the Vatican and Palestine), the same way every other count on this site works.

If you want to count them yourself, that is a legitimate choice. Just be consistent, and say which rule you used. We explain the options in [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country).

## Why do other lists say a different number?

The number changes with three choices.

1. **Sovereign or not.** Count only UN members and you get 14. Add the Cook Islands and Niue and you get 16.
2. **Where Oceania stops.** Some lists include Indonesia's Papua provinces or Hawaii (a US state) as part of Oceania. Hawaii is a state of the United States, so it counts as part of the USA, not as a separate country.
3. **Regions versus continents.** Outside the English-speaking world, Oceania is usually treated as a continent. In the English-speaking world, Australia is sometimes treated as its own continent and the islands as "the Pacific". That changes how a list is titled, not how many sovereign states there are.

If you see a number that is not 14 or 16, check which of these a list used.

## How do you count Oceania on a travel map?

Here is a rule that is easy to follow and easy to defend.

1. Count the 14 UN-member countries.
2. Treat territories as places you have been, but do not add them to your country total.
3. If you land in a territory, mark it anyway. It is still part of your story.

On Tripfolio's [world map](/countries-visited-map?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=how-many-countries-in-oceania) you mark each place as visited or want to visit. Only the 195 sovereign countries count toward your "x / 195", your percentage of the world and your milestones. Territories are colored in but not counted. ## Which Oceania country should you visit first?

There is no right answer, but here is a short way to think about it.

- **Easy first trip:** Australia or New Zealand. Big, well connected, and simple to plan.
- **Beach and island trip:** Fiji, Tonga or Samoa are popular island picks.
- **Remote:** Tuvalu, Kiribati, Nauru and Marshall Islands are small and far from most travelers' routes, so check how to get there well ahead.

Whatever you choose, add the rest to your wishlist. Seeing 14 countries listed in front of you is a good reminder of how much of the Pacific most travelers never see.

## FAQ

### How many countries are in Oceania?

There are 14 sovereign countries in Oceania. All are members of the United Nations. The region also has about a dozen territories that belong to other countries.

### Is Australia a country or a continent?

Australia is a country. It is also the main landmass that some people call a continent. Outside the English-speaking world, Oceania as a whole is usually treated as the continent.

### Are the Cook Islands and Niue countries?

They are self-governing states in free association with New Zealand, but they are not UN members, so most lists, including ours, treat them as territories. Some travelers count them separately, which is fine if you say so.

### Is Hawaii part of Oceania?

Geographically Hawaii is in Polynesia, but it is a US state, so it counts as part of the United States and not as a country of Oceania.

### What is the smallest country in Oceania?

Nauru is the smallest country in Oceania by area, a single island in Micronesia. Our [smallest countries guide](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world) has the details.
