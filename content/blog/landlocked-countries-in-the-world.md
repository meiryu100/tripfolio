There are **44 landlocked countries** in the world: countries with no coastline on an ocean or open sea. Africa has the most (16), followed by Europe (14), Asia (12) and South America (2). North America and Oceania have none. Two of the 44, Liechtenstein and Uzbekistan, are "doubly landlocked", which means every neighbor is landlocked too.

This guide gives the full list of landlocked countries by continent, explains the doubly landlocked ones and the border cases, and shows how to track them on a travel map if you like collecting countries.

## How many landlocked countries are there?

[Wikipedia's article on landlocked countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) counts 44 landlocked countries, plus three landlocked de facto states that are not widely recognized: Kosovo, South Ossetia and Transnistria. That makes 47 if you include them, and some sources quote higher totals that add other partially recognized places, so always check what a list counts.

The number has not always been 44. Wikipedia notes that the count has grown from 30 landlocked countries since 1990, mostly through the breakup of the Soviet Union and Yugoslavia and through new borders in Africa.

## What is the full list of landlocked countries?

Here are all 44, grouped by continent using the United Nations-style geographic grouping used by Wikipedia.

### Landlocked countries in Africa (16)

Botswana, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Central African Republic, Chad, Eswatini, Ethiopia, Lesotho, Malawi, Mali, Niger, Rwanda, South Sudan, Uganda, Zambia and Zimbabwe.

Our guide to [how many countries are in Africa](/blog/how-many-countries-in-africa) explains the continent's 54 sovereign countries, so almost three in ten African countries are landlocked.

### Landlocked countries in Europe (14)

Andorra, Austria, Belarus, Czechia, Hungary, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Moldova, North Macedonia, San Marino, Serbia, Slovakia, Switzerland and Vatican City.

Wikipedia lists the Czech Republic under its older short name; it is the same country Tripfolio shows as Czechia. Several of these are tiny, and our list of the [smallest countries in the world](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world) has more on Vatican City, San Marino, Liechtenstein and Andorra. For the wider picture, see [how many countries are in Europe](/blog/how-many-countries-in-europe).

### Landlocked countries in Asia (12)

Afghanistan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bhutan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Laos, Mongolia, Nepal, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan.

Some of these are border cases for continent labels. Armenia and Azerbaijan, for example, are sometimes grouped with Europe, and Kazakhstan straddles both continents. If you count by continent, check which grouping your list uses. Our guide on [how many countries are in Asia](/blog/how-many-countries-in-asia) shows how we handle this.

### Landlocked countries in South America (2)

Bolivia and Paraguay. They are the only two landlocked countries in the Americas.

## Which countries are doubly landlocked?

A doubly landlocked country is one surrounded entirely by landlocked countries, so you would have to cross at least two borders to reach any coast. Wikipedia names two:

- **Liechtenstein**, surrounded by Austria and Switzerland.
- **Uzbekistan**, surrounded by Afghanistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan. It became doubly landlocked in 1991, when the Soviet Union dissolved.

There is one catch. Uzbekistan counts as doubly landlocked only if the Caspian Sea is treated as a lake. If the Caspian is treated as a sea, then Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan have a coastline and Uzbekistan is not doubly landlocked. This is why you will sometimes see "the only doubly landlocked country" and other times a list of two.

## What is the largest landlocked country?

By area, **Kazakhstan** is the largest landlocked country in the world at about 2.72 million km², according to Wikipedia. Mongolia (about 1.57 million km²) is second and Chad (about 1.28 million km²) is third. Kazakhstan is also in the top ten of our guide to the [largest countries in the world](/blog/largest-countries-in-the-world).

By population, **Ethiopia** is the largest, with about 113.7 million people in the figures Wikipedia cites. Uganda and Uzbekistan follow. Population figures change quickly, so check a national statistics office if you need a current number.

## Which landlocked country has the most neighbors?

Wikipedia's table shows a three-way tie at eight neighbors: **Austria**, **Serbia** and **Zambia**. That makes them good picks for border-hopping trips, and a good fit for ideas in [countries you can visit in one trip](/blog/countries-you-can-visit-in-one-trip).

## How did some countries become landlocked?

A country is landlocked by geography or by history. A few well-known cases from Wikipedia:

- **Bolivia** lost its coastline to Chile in the War of the Pacific (1879 to 1884), and the loss was accepted in treaties in 1884 and 1904.
- **Ethiopia** became landlocked when Eritrea gained independence after a 30-year war.
- **Serbia** became landlocked in 2006, when Montenegro left the state union.
- **South Sudan** was already landlocked as part of Sudan, and stayed landlocked when it became independent in 2011.

Landlocked status matters for trade. The United Nations classifies 32 of the 44 landlocked countries as Landlocked Developing Countries, a group with its own UN programs, because they depend on neighbors' ports and roads to reach world markets.

## How do landlocked countries count on a travel map?

All 44 are sovereign countries, with two details worth knowing if you track visits:

- **Kosovo** is a landlocked de facto state in the Wikipedia list, but Tripfolio treats it as a territory, not a sovereign country. It appears on the map, but does not count toward the "x / 195" total. Our guide to [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world) explains the 195 count, and the post on [countries that aren't in the UN](/blog/countries-that-arent-in-the-un) covers the edge cases.
- **Transnistria** and **South Ossetia** are not separate countries on the map.

So a complete "landlocked set" on Tripfolio is the 44 sovereign countries above. A fun challenge is to see how many you have visited. Many travelers have been to Switzerland, Austria or the Czech Republic, while Bhutan, Mali or Paraguay sit unticked for years. Our country pages for [Switzerland](/countries/switzerland), [Mongolia](/countries/mongolia) and [Bolivia](/countries/bolivia) are good starting points.

If you want a rule for what "visited" means, read [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country). Transit through a landlocked country's airport is a classic gray area.

## FAQ

### How many landlocked countries are there in the world?

There are 44 landlocked countries. Including three landlocked de facto states (Kosovo, South Ossetia and Transnistria), the total is 47.

### Which continent has the most landlocked countries?

Africa, with 16. Europe has 14, Asia 12 and South America 2. North America and Oceania have none.

### Which countries are doubly landlocked?

Liechtenstein and Uzbekistan. Every country that borders them is landlocked too, though Uzbekistan's status depends on whether the Caspian Sea is treated as a lake.

### What is the largest landlocked country?

Kazakhstan, at about 2.72 million km². Mongolia and Chad are second and third.

### Is Switzerland landlocked?

Yes. Switzerland is surrounded by Germany, France, Italy, Austria and Liechtenstein, and has no sea coast.
