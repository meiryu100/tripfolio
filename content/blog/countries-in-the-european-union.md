There are **27 countries in the European Union** as of 2026. The founding six were Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Luxembourg and the Netherlands, and the most recent member to join was Croatia in 2013. The United Kingdom left on 31 January 2020, the only member state ever to withdraw. Below you'll find the full list, the year each country joined, which ones use the euro, and why "EU country" is not the same as "European country".

## How did the EU grow from 6 to 27 countries?

The [European Commission's enlargement history](https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/enlargement-policy/history-enlargement-6-27-members_en) shows the waves:

- **Founding six:** Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Luxembourg and the Netherlands.
- **1973:** Denmark, Ireland and the United Kingdom joined.
- **1981:** Greece.
- **1986:** Spain and Portugal.
- **1995:** Austria, Finland and Sweden.
- **2004:** the biggest single wave, ten countries: Cyprus, Czechia, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Slovakia and Slovenia.
- **2007:** Bulgaria and Romania.
- **2013:** Croatia.

The Commission's page also notes that the United Kingdom has not been part of the EU since 1 February 2020 (Wikipedia gives the exit date as 31 January 2020). With the UK gone, the count fell from 28 to 27.

## How many EU countries use the euro?

Twenty-one of the 27 use the euro, based on the currency of each member listed on Wikipedia. Bulgaria is the newest, with the EU's own page showing 2026 as its euro year. The six members that keep their own currency are:

- Czechia (koruna)
- Denmark (krone)
- Hungary (forint)
- Poland (złoty)
- Romania (leu)
- Sweden (krona)

Euro membership is a separate list from EU membership: a country can be in the EU without the euro. For a trip, this just means you'll still need some local cash in those six.

## Is EU the same as Europe, or the Schengen Area?

No, and this trips up a lot of travelers. Three lists overlap but differ:

- **European countries:** the UN geoscheme counts 44 sovereign states in Europe, 45 with Cyprus as Tripfolio does. See [how many countries are in Europe](/blog/how-many-countries-in-europe).
- **EU members:** 27.
- **Schengen Area:** the border-free travel zone. It is not the same as the EU: Ireland isn't in it, while Norway, Iceland, Switzerland and Liechtenstein are. Our guide to [countries you can visit in one trip](/blog/countries-you-can-visit-in-one-trip) explains how that helps you tick off several countries at once.

Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, the United Kingdom, Ukraine, Serbia and Albania are all in Europe but not in the EU. The UK, Norway and Switzerland are the ones people most often assume are members.

## Which EU countries are the best to start with?

If you're building your map from scratch, the EU is one of the easiest places to collect countries, because borders are open and trains are frequent. A few good starting points, each with its own page showing what the country offers and who has been:

- [France](/countries/france), the most obvious first stop
- [Germany](/countries/germany), with many neighbors a short train ride away
- [Luxembourg](/countries/luxembourg), a small member that is easy to add from Belgium, France or Germany
- [Malta](/countries/malta), the smallest EU member by population (about half a million people, according to Wikipedia)

For the other end of the scale, Germany is the most populous member, with roughly 83 to 84 million people. Wikipedia's two figures differ slightly, so treat that as a range.

## What about territories of EU countries?

This is where counting gets interesting. Some EU members have regions far from Europe. Wikipedia lists "outermost regions" that are legally part of the EU: the Azores and Madeira (Portugal), the Canary Islands (Spain), and French Guiana, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Mayotte, Réunion and Saint Martin (France). French Guiana is in South America, and Réunion and Mayotte are in the Indian Ocean.

Do they count as extra countries? For a personal count, no. They belong to their parent country. Tripfolio follows the same logic: only the 195 sovereign countries count toward your total, and territories can be colored on the map but aren't added to the score. We explain the reasoning in [what is a dependent territory](/blog/what-is-a-dependent-territory) and [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country).

## FAQ

### How many countries are in the European Union?

Twenty-seven. The number has been 27 since the United Kingdom left on 31 January 2020; Croatia, which joined in 2013, remains the newest member.

### Which countries are not in the EU but in Europe?

Examples include Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, the United Kingdom, Ukraine, Serbia and Albania. Europe has about 44 to 46 countries, depending on how you count Cyprus and Kosovo.

### Which EU countries don't use the euro?

Czechia, Denmark, Hungary, Poland, Romania and Sweden keep their own currencies. The other 21 members use the euro, with Bulgaria the most recent to adopt it.

### Which country left the European Union?

The United Kingdom, after a June 2016 referendum. It joined in 1973 and left on 31 January 2020, and no other member state has ever withdrawn.

### Is Switzerland in the EU?

No. Switzerland is in the Schengen Area but is not an EU member.
