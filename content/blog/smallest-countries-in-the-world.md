The smallest country in the world is Vatican City, at roughly half a square kilometer, followed by Monaco at about 2 km², Nauru at 21 km², Tuvalu at 26 km² and San Marino at 61 km². Together with ten more, they make up the 15 smallest sovereign countries, and five of them sit in Europe, where adding a tiny country to your count can take an afternoon.

This guide ranks the smallest countries in the world by total area, shows which continent each one is on, and points out which are the easiest to fit into a trip you were already taking.

## What are the 15 smallest countries in the world?

Here is the ranking by total area, using the figures in [Wikipedia's list of countries and dependencies by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area), which takes its totals mainly from United Nations Statistics Division data. Sources differ slightly on the very smallest places, so treat the numbers as close approximations.

1. **Vatican City**: about 0.44 km² (Europe)
2. **Monaco**: about 2 km² (Europe)
3. **Nauru**: 21 km² (Oceania)
4. **Tuvalu**: 26 km² (Oceania)
5. **San Marino**: 61 km² (Europe)
6. **Liechtenstein**: 160 km² (Europe)
7. **Marshall Islands**: 181 km² (Oceania)
8. **Saint Kitts and Nevis**: 261 km² (North America, Caribbean)
9. **Maldives**: about 300 km² (Asia)
10. **Malta**: 315 km² (Europe)
11. **Grenada**: 345 km² (North America, Caribbean)
12. **Saint Vincent and the Grenadines**: 389 km² (North America, Caribbean)
13. **Barbados**: 431 km² (North America, Caribbean)
14. **Antigua and Barbuda**: 442 km² (North America, Caribbean)
15. **Seychelles**: 457 km² (Africa)

Continents follow the way Tripfolio files them: five in Europe, five in the Caribbean (part of North America), three in Oceania, and one each in Asia and Africa.

## Is Vatican City really a country?

For counting purposes, yes, and Tripfolio counts it. Vatican City is an independent state, and the Holy See has a seat at the United Nations as a permanent observer rather than a full member, according to the [UN's list of non-member states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/non-member-states). That is why traveler counts usually say 195 countries rather than the 193 UN member states: the two observer states, the Holy See (Vatican City) and Palestine, are added on top. Our guide to [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world) explains the different totals.

Vatican City's area is quoted as anywhere from about 0.44 to 0.49 km² depending on the source, so the exact figure varies from list to list. Either way, it is the smallest by a wide margin.

## Which tiny countries are easiest to add to your count?

The easiest ones are the European microstates, because you can reach them from a larger neighbor without a dedicated trip.

### Vatican City

Per [Wikipedia's Vatican City article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City), visitors coming from the surrounding Italian territory face no passport controls, and St. Peter's Square, separated from Italy only by a white line, and the Basilica are free to enter. Anyone spending a day in Rome can reasonably add it. If you wonder whether standing in the square is enough to count, read [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country). Our [Vatican City page](/countries/vatican-city) has the basics.

### Monaco

Monaco is surrounded by France on land, with the Mediterranean to the south, so it is an easy add-on to a trip along the French Riviera. See the [Monaco page](/countries/monaco).

### San Marino and Liechtenstein

San Marino is enclosed by Italy, and Liechtenstein sits between Switzerland and Austria, so both are natural add-ons to a trip in a neighboring country. Check current transport options before you go, since not every route is frequent.

### Malta

Unlike the others, Malta is an island nation in the Mediterranean, so it needs a flight or ferry. It is compact, which makes it a good long weekend. Read more on the [Malta page](/countries/malta).

For the full European picture, including which microstates count, see [how many countries are in Europe](/blog/how-many-countries-in-europe).

## Which of the smallest countries are islands?

Eleven of the 15 are island nations: Nauru, Tuvalu, the Marshall Islands, Saint Kitts and Nevis, the Maldives, Malta, Grenada, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Barbados, Antigua and Barbuda and Seychelles. Only Vatican City, Monaco, San Marino and Liechtenstein are on a continental mainland.

Islands are often the hardest to reach cheaply, but the Caribbean five are close neighbors, so combining more than one on a single trip is realistic. The Pacific trio of Nauru, Tuvalu and the Marshall Islands is spread across a vast ocean, so plan those well ahead and confirm current flight schedules with the airlines.

## Does a tiny country count the same as a big one?

On a country-counting map, yes. Tripfolio counts the 195 sovereign countries toward your "x / 195" total, so Vatican City or Nauru is worth exactly as much as Russia or Brazil. Territories and dependencies are colored on the map but not counted toward the total.

That is why many counters go for the microstates first: for a trip that was already planned, they are the cheapest way to move your number up. If you are unsure whether a short stop qualifies, decide on one rule and apply it consistently, as described in our guide on [what counts as visiting](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country).

## FAQ

### What is the smallest country in the world?

Vatican City, at roughly 0.44 to 0.49 km² depending on the source. It lies inside Rome, Italy.

### What is the smallest country in Africa?

Of the 15 smallest countries in the world, Seychelles (457 km²) is the only one in Africa, so it is the continent's smallest on this list.

### Is Monaco the second-smallest country?

Yes. At about 2 km² it is the second-smallest sovereign country, though it is several times larger than Vatican City.

### Is the Maldives one of the smallest countries?

Yes. At about 300 km² it ranks ninth and is the only Asian country in the smallest 15.
