There are **13 countries in the Caribbean**. That is the number of sovereign states among the 28 islands and island groups the United Nations files under "Caribbean". The other 15 are territories that belong to the United States, France, the Netherlands or the United Kingdom, so they are not countries on their own. Lists that say 16 or 20 or 30 are counting something different, usually mainland neighbors or territories.

This guide gives you the 13 countries, the 15 territories, the reason the numbers vary and a simple way to count the Caribbean on your own travel map.

## How many countries are in the Caribbean?

The United Nations Statistics Division groups the world into regions in its [standard country and area codes](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/). Its Caribbean group has **28 entries**. Of those:

- **13 are sovereign states**, and all of them are UN members.
- **15 are territories**, such as Puerto Rico, Aruba and the Cayman Islands.

So the honest answer has two layers: 13 countries, and 28 places if you include everything with its own name on the map. That is the same rule we use for the world total in [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world): a sovereign country counts, a dependency does not.

5. **Dominica**, Roseau
6. **Dominican Republic**, Santo Domingo
7. **Grenada**, St. George's

9. **Jamaica**, Kingston
10. **Saint Kitts and Nevis**, Basseterre
11. **Saint Lucia**, Castries
12. **Saint Vincent and the Grenadines**, Kingstown
13. **Trinidad and Tobago**, Port of Spain

Three of them are one country made of several islands: Antigua and Barbuda, Saint Kitts and Nevis, and Trinidad and Tobago. Cuba, Haiti and the Dominican Republic are the three big ones, and Haiti and the Dominican Republic share the island of Hispaniola. Visiting two islands of the same country is one country on your map, not two.

## Which Caribbean places are territories?

The UN list has 15 territories in the Caribbean. Each one is tied to another country:

### United States

- Puerto Rico
- United States Virgin Islands

### France

- Martinique
- Saint Barthélemy
- Saint Martin (the French half of the island)

### Netherlands

- Aruba
- Curaçao

### United Kingdom

- Anguilla
- British Virgin Islands
- Cayman Islands
- Montserrat
- Turks and Caicos Islands

## Why do some lists give a different number?

Four things move the total.

### Territories are counted as countries

Many travel sites say "Aruba is a country" because it has its own flag, passport stamp and tourism board. Aruba is a constituent country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands, not a sovereign state. If you count all 28 places, you will get 28. If you count only independent states, you get 13.

### Mainland neighbors are added

Belize, Guyana and Suriname sit on the mainland but have a Caribbean coast or Caribbean ties. The UN does not put them in its Caribbean group: it files Belize under Central America and Guyana under South America. Adding them is how some lists reach 16.

### Bermuda and other Atlantic islands are added

Bermuda is often sold as a Caribbean trip, but it lies in the North Atlantic, well to the north. The UN lists it under Northern America, not the Caribbean.

### CARICOM is mistaken for the region

The Caribbean Community, [CARICOM](https://caricom.org/our-community/who-we-are/), says it has 15 member states and 8 associate members. It is a political and trade group, not a map of the region, and its membership does not match the UN's Caribbean list. Do not use its number as a country count.

## Which number should I use for my own count?

Pick one rule and keep it:

1. **13, sovereign states only.** The cleanest, and the one Tripfolio uses. It lines up with the world total of 195.
2. **13 plus territories.** Count the 13 as countries and keep Puerto Rico, Aruba and the rest as bonus places beside them.
3. **A personal rule.** Some travelers count a place only if they stayed a night or left the airport. If you are not sure where you stand, read [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country).

The Caribbean is a good example of why this matters. A single week-long cruise can touch five or six ports, several of them territories. Under the sovereign-states rule that might add two countries to your list, not six. For the bigger picture of turning your list into a percentage, see [how many countries you have visited](/blog/how-many-countries-have-i-visited).

## FAQ

### How many countries are in the Caribbean?

There are 13 sovereign countries in the Caribbean. The UN lists 28 places in the region in total, and the other 15 are territories of the United States, France, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom.

### Is Puerto Rico a country?

No. Puerto Rico is a territory of the United States. It is not on the list of 13 Caribbean countries, but you can mark it on Tripfolio's map. It does not count toward your total out of 195.

### Is Aruba a country?

Aruba is a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands, not a sovereign state. It is not a UN member, so it is counted as a territory.

### Are Belize and Guyana in the Caribbean?

Not by the UN's grouping. The UN lists Belize under Central America and Guyana under South America, even though both are linked to the Caribbean through history and CARICOM membership.

### Is Bermuda in the Caribbean?

No. Bermuda is in the North Atlantic, and the UN lists it under Northern America, not the Caribbean.
