The Commonwealth of Nations has **56 member countries**. They are the United Kingdom plus 55 other independent states, from Canada and India to Tuvalu and Vanuatu. Here is the full Commonwealth countries list, grouped by region, with the year each country joined. The count and the regions come from the [Commonwealth's own member page](https://thecommonwealth.org/our-member-countries), and the join years from Wikipedia's [member states of the Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Commonwealth_of_Nations) page.

If you count countries on a map, this is a handy group to know. It spans every inhabited continent except Antarctica, so a Commonwealth checklist takes you almost everywhere.

## What is the Commonwealth, exactly?

The Commonwealth is a voluntary association of independent countries. It is not a country, and it has no power to make laws for its members. King Charles III is the Head of the Commonwealth, a symbolic role. The Commonwealth Secretariat in London does the day-to-day work, led by a Secretary-General.

Two numbers are easy to mix up:

- **56 members** are the full Commonwealth, and every one of them is a sovereign country.
- **15 Commonwealth realms** are the members that share the monarch as head of state, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. The other members are republics or have their own monarchs.

The Commonwealth says about 2.8 billion people live in its member countries, and that 33 of the world's 42 small states are members, meaning countries of 1.5 million people or fewer. That is why the list has so many islands.

## Commonwealth countries by region

The Commonwealth groups its members into five regions. Join years are in brackets.

### Africa (21)

Botswana (1966), Cameroon (1995), Gabon (2022), The Gambia (1965), Ghana (1957), Kenya (1963), Eswatini (1968), Lesotho (1966), Malawi (1964), Mauritius (1968), Mozambique (1995), Namibia (1990), Nigeria (1960), Rwanda (2009), Seychelles (1976), Sierra Leone (1961), South Africa (1926), Togo (2022), Tanzania (1961), Uganda (1962) and Zambia (1964).

Africa has the most members of any region, and more than a third of the whole Commonwealth. If you are working through the continent, our guide to [how many countries are in Africa](/blog/how-many-countries-in-africa) has the full list of 54.

### Asia (8)

Bangladesh (1972), Brunei (1984), India (1947), Malaysia (1957), Maldives (1982), Pakistan (1947), Singapore (1965) and Sri Lanka (1948).

You can read about [India](/countries/india), [Singapore](/countries/singapore) and the others on their country pages.

### Caribbean and Americas (13)

Antigua and Barbuda (1981), Bahamas (1973), Barbados (1966), Belize (1981), Canada (1926), Dominica (1978), Grenada (1974), Guyana (1966), Jamaica (1962), Saint Lucia (1979), Saint Kitts and Nevis (1983), Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (1979) and Trinidad and Tobago (1962).

Most of these are Caribbean islands. Belize sits on the Central American mainland, Guyana is on the South American mainland, and Canada is the only large country in the group. For the island chain, see [how many countries are in the Caribbean](/blog/how-many-countries-in-the-caribbean).

### Europe (3)

Cyprus (1961), Malta (1964) and the United Kingdom (1926).

Only three European countries are members. To compare with another European club, see [which countries are in the European Union](/blog/countries-in-the-european-union).

### Pacific (11)

Australia (1926), Fiji (1970), Kiribati (1979), Nauru (1968), New Zealand (1926), Papua New Guinea (1975), Samoa (1970), Solomon Islands (1978), Tonga (1970), Tuvalu (1978) and Vanuatu (1980).

[Australia](/countries/australia) is by far the largest of the 11.

## Which countries joined most recently?

**Gabon and Togo** joined in June 2022, which makes them the newest members. Before them, Rwanda joined in 2009.

Three members stand out because they have no past constitutional link to the UK:

- **Mozambique** joined in 1995 and was the first.
- **Rwanda** joined in 2009 as the second.
- **Gabon** joined in 2022 as the third.

## Which countries left, and which came back?

Membership is not permanent. Four cases come up most often:

- **Ireland** joined as the Irish Free State in 1926. The Commonwealth dates its departure to 18 April 1949, while the Irish government dates it to 1936. It is not a member today.
- **Zimbabwe** joined in 1980, was suspended in 2002 and withdrew on 7 December 2003. It applied to rejoin in 2018, and as of the Wikipedia page we used it had not been readmitted.
- **The Gambia** withdrew on 3 October 2013 and rejoined on 8 February 2018.
- **The Maldives** withdrew on 13 October 2016 and rejoined on 1 February 2020.

So if you see a list with 53 or 54 countries, it is probably an older one. The count has grown over the years, and Gabon and Togo took it to 56.

## How do Commonwealth countries show up on a world map?

Here is how the 56 fall out when we sort them by the continents used on the Tripfolio map. Tripfolio counts **195 sovereign countries** in total, and every Commonwealth member is one of them:

- Africa: 21
- North America: 12
- Oceania: 11
- Asia: 8
- Europe: 3
- South America: 1 (Guyana)

That is 56 in total. Notice that this differs from the Commonwealth's own regions above. The Commonwealth puts Belize and Guyana in "Caribbean and Americas", while on our map Belize is in North America and Guyana is in South America. Cyprus is in Europe on our map, even though the UN's geographic scheme files it under Asia. Check [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) if you want to decide your own rules.

One more map detail: the Commonwealth has only independent states. British Overseas Territories such as Bermuda or the Cayman Islands are not members, and they do not count toward the 195 on Tripfolio either. Tripfolio colors them, but only the 195 sovereign countries feed your "x / 195" number.

Two Commonwealth members are also split into regions on the Tripfolio map. Each has its own guide: [Canada's provinces and territories](/blog/canada-provinces-visited-map) and [Australia's states and territories](/blog/australian-states-visited).

## FAQ

### How many countries are in the Commonwealth?

There are 56 member countries as of 2026, according to the Commonwealth's own member page. Gabon and Togo, which joined in 2022, are the newest.

### Which countries are in the Commonwealth of Nations?

The 56 members are spread over Africa (21), the Caribbean and Americas (13), the Pacific (11), Asia (8) and Europe (3). The full list with join years is above.

### Is the United States in the Commonwealth?

No. The United States is not a member of the Commonwealth.

### Is Ireland in the Commonwealth?

No. Ireland joined as the Irish Free State in 1926 and left in 1949, according to the Commonwealth, although the Irish government dates its departure to 1936.

### Can a country that was never British join the Commonwealth?

Yes. Mozambique (1995), Rwanda (2009) and Gabon (2022) joined without a past constitutional link to the UK.
