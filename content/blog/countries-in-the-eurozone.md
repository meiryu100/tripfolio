There are **21 countries in the eurozone** as of 2026. All of them are members of the European Union, and the newest is Bulgaria, which adopted the euro in 2026. Six more countries use the euro without being members: Andorra, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City by agreement, and Montenegro and Kosovo on their own. Below is the full list, the year each country joined, and what it means when you are counting countries on a map.

## Which countries are in the eurozone?

The [European Central Bank](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/intro/html/index.en.html) says euro banknotes and coins are legal tender in 21 of the 27 EU member states. In alphabetical order, with the year each adopted the euro (all on 1 January), based on [Wikipedia's Eurozone article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone):

1. Austria (1999)
2. Belgium (1999)
3. Bulgaria (2026)
4. Croatia (2023)
5. Cyprus (2008)
6. Estonia (2011)
7. Finland (1999)
8. France (1999)
9. Germany (1999)
10. Greece (2001)
11. Ireland (1999)
12. Italy (1999)
13. Latvia (2014)
14. Lithuania (2015)
15. Luxembourg (1999)
16. Malta (2008)
17. Netherlands (1999)
18. Portugal (1999)
19. Slovakia (2009)
20. Slovenia (2007)
21. Spain (1999)

"Eurozone" and "euro area" mean the same thing. Both describe the countries whose currency is the euro and whose monetary policy is set by the ECB.

## How did the eurozone grow from 11 to 21 countries?

The currency launched on 1 January 1999 with eleven states: Austria, Belgium, Finland, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal and Spain. Euro banknotes and coins only entered circulation on 1 January 2002, so for the first three years the euro existed as an accounting currency. Here are the waves after the start:

- **2001:** Greece
- **2007:** Slovenia
- **2008:** Cyprus and Malta
- **2009:** Slovakia
- **2011:** Estonia
- **2014:** Latvia
- **2015:** Lithuania
- **2023:** Croatia
- **2026:** Bulgaria

That makes 11 founders plus ten later joiners, which adds up to 21. Wikipedia's page is inconsistent on the size of the founding group (its own table shows eleven), so the table is the number to trust.

## Which EU countries don't use the euro?

Six EU members keep their own currency:

- Czechia
- Denmark
- Hungary
- Poland
- Romania
- Sweden

Denmark has a treaty opt-out, so it is legally exempt from adopting the euro unless its government decides otherwise. The other five are committed to adopting it once they meet the convergence criteria. Sweden is a special case: it rejected the euro in a 2003 referendum and has avoided the requirements by staying out of ERM II, the exchange-rate mechanism a country normally joins for at least two years before adopting the euro. Poland's government has said it is not economically ready.

If you are planning a trip, this is the practical takeaway: in those six you will still want some local cash or a card that handles the currency. Our guide to the [countries in the European Union](/blog/countries-in-the-european-union) has the full list of 27 with their join years.

## Which countries use the euro without being in the eurozone?

Six countries use the euro but are not members of the eurozone, according to the ECB:

- **Andorra, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City** use it under formal arrangements with the European Community. They issue their own euro coins but have no seat in the ECB or the Eurogroup.
- **Montenegro and Kosovo** adopted the euro unilaterally, with no formal agreement. They have no right to issue coins and no representation in eurozone institutions.

So if the question is "where can I pay in euros?", the answer is 27 countries. If it is "which countries are in the eurozone?", the answer is 21. Some French overseas territories also use the euro, including Saint Barthélemy and Saint-Pierre and Miquelon. Sint Maarten, by contrast, does not: it uses a guilder pegged to the US dollar.

## How big is the eurozone?

Wikipedia's infobox, quoting Eurostat, gives a population of about 359.6 million on 1 January 2026. It puts the eurozone's nominal GDP at roughly €16 trillion for 2025. Inside the zone the countries are very different in size. In 2024 Germany had about 83.4 million people and France about 68.4 million, while Malta had about 563,000 and Luxembourg about 672,000. Those are 2024 figures from the same Wikipedia table, so treat them as approximate.

## Is the eurozone the same as the EU or Schengen?

No. Three lists overlap, and travelers mix them up all the time:

- **EU:** 27 countries.
- **Eurozone:** 21 countries, all of them in the EU.
- **Schengen Area:** the border-free travel zone, which includes non-EU countries such as Norway, Iceland and Switzerland, while Ireland, an EU and eurozone member, stays outside it.

Ireland is the clearest example of why the lists differ: it uses the euro but is not part of Schengen. ## FAQ

### How many countries are in the eurozone?

Twenty-one. Bulgaria was the most recent to adopt the euro, in 2026. Six more countries use the euro without being members.

### Which EU countries are not in the eurozone?

Czechia, Denmark, Hungary, Poland, Romania and Sweden. Denmark has a formal opt-out, and the other five are expected to adopt the euro once they meet the criteria.

### Is the UK in the eurozone?

No. The United Kingdom left the EU on 31 January 2020 and uses the pound. Only EU members can be in the eurozone.

### Which countries use the euro but are not in the eurozone?

Andorra, Monaco, San Marino, Vatican City, Montenegro and Kosovo. The first four have formal agreements, while Montenegro and Kosovo adopted it unilaterally.

### Is Switzerland in the eurozone?

No. Switzerland is not an EU member and uses its own franc.
