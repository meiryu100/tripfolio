Australia has **6 states and 2 mainland territories**, 8 places in total, and tracking your Australian states visited means ticking off each of the eight you've actually been to. The states are New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania. The two territories are the Australian Capital Territory (home of Canberra) and the Northern Territory.

Most travelers who tour Australia see two or three of them. Finishing all eight is a real project, because the distances are huge. This guide lists every state and territory with its capital, shows how different they are in size, suggests a fair rule for what counts as visited, and explains how to turn the result into a map you can share.

## What are the 6 states and 2 territories of Australia?

According to [Wikipedia's article on the states and territories of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_Australia), here is the full list with capitals:

### The 6 states

- **New South Wales**: capital Sydney
- **Victoria**: capital Melbourne
- **Queensland**: capital Brisbane
- **Western Australia**: capital Perth
- **South Australia**: capital Adelaide
- **Tasmania**: capital Hobart

All six states came together in the federation of 1901.

### The 2 territories

- **Australian Capital Territory (ACT)**: capital Canberra, self-governing since 1989
- **Northern Territory (NT)**: capital Darwin, self-governing since 1978

Canberra, the national capital, sits inside the ACT, so it is the one capital that is not in a state. Sydney is the largest city in New South Wales, but it is not the national capital, which surprises many first-time visitors.

## Are there more than 8? The "other territories" question

Strictly speaking, yes. The same Wikipedia article counts 10 federal territories: 3 internal ones on the mainland (the ACT, the Northern Territory and the Jervis Bay Territory) and 7 offshore external ones. The Australian Bureau of Statistics also notes that its national total includes the "Other Territories": [Jervis Bay Territory, Christmas Island, the Cocos (Keeling) Islands and Norfolk Island](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/national-state-and-territory-population/latest-release).

For a travel map, the usual convention is the simple one: **6 states + 2 territories = 8**. That is also how Tripfolio's map splits Australia. Christmas Island, the Cocos (Keeling) Islands and Norfolk Island are listed in Tripfolio as separate territories on the world map, and like all territories they are colored but not counted toward the 195. If you've been to one of them, mark it on its own.

## How big are they? The numbers behind the distances

Size is the reason this list takes years. These are the figures from Wikipedia's states and territories page, with population estimates for 31 March 2026 that match the [Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/national-state-and-territory-population/latest-release):

- **Largest by area:** Western Australia, about 2.53 million km².
- **Smallest by area:** the Australian Capital Territory, about 2,358 km². Western Australia is more than a thousand times bigger.
- **Most people:** New South Wales, about 8.67 million.
- **Fewest people:** the Northern Territory, about 268,900, spread over about 1.35 million km².
- **Smallest state:** Tasmania, about 68,400 km² and about 580,700 people.

So the eight are very different challenges. The ACT is small enough to be a short trip from Sydney or Melbourne. Western Australia and the Northern Territory are the opposite: huge, thinly populated and far from the east-coast cities where most itineraries start.

## What counts as "visited" in Australia?

There is no official rule, so choose one and keep it. Our longer guide on [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) walks through the options, and the same ideas apply to states:

- **Airport only.** Changing planes in Melbourne on the way to Tasmania, or in Brisbane on the way somewhere else, doesn't give you Victoria or Queensland. Most travelers don't count it.
- **Driving through.** Crossing a border on a road trip and stopping only for fuel is a gray area. A fair test is that you stopped for something besides petrol.
- **Overnight.** The cleanest rule: you slept there. It is strict for the territories, but it makes your map one you can defend.
- **As a child.** If your family took you to the Gold Coast at age six and you remember only the beach, most people still count it. It's your map.

A practical tip: famous places sit inside one state, and it's easy to lose track of which. Uluru is in the Northern Territory, the Great Barrier Reef is off Queensland, and the Sydney Opera House is in New South Wales. Whatever you decide, apply the same rule to every state, so the final number means something.

## How to plan the states you're missing

Once you have your list, the gaps usually fall into groups:

1. **The east-coast trio.** New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland hold the biggest cities. Most itineraries cover at least two of them.
2. **The easy add-ons.** The ACT is a short trip from Sydney, and Tasmania is an island state reached by a short flight or ferry from the mainland. Each is often a long weekend.
3. **South Australia.** Adelaide is easy to skip because it isn't on the classic Sydney, Melbourne and Cairns route, but it is a natural stop on a trip between the east and the center.
4. **The far west and the north.** Western Australia and the Northern Territory need dedicated time. Put them on the wishlist side of your map rather than feeling behind.

If you are building a trip around the gaps, check which states you've already done before booking. Filling the map in order of difficulty is more satisfying than leaving the two hardest for last and never going.

## FAQ

### How many states and territories does Australia have?

Australia has 6 states and 2 mainland territories, 8 in total. The states are New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania. The territories are the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory.

### What is the largest state in Australia?

Western Australia is the largest, at about 2.53 million km². The smallest state is Tasmania, at about 68,400 km², and the smallest of all eight is the Australian Capital Territory.

### What is the difference between a state and a territory in Australia?

The six states joined in the 1901 federation. The ACT and the Northern Territory are self-governing territories, from 1989 and 1978 respectively. For a traveler there is no difference: both appear on the map and both count as places visited.

### Is Canberra in a state?

No. Canberra is the national capital and lies inside the Australian Capital Territory, which is a territory, not a state. Sydney, the biggest city, is the capital of New South Wales.

### Does visiting another Australian state count as a new country?

No. States and territories are part of Australia, so visiting Victoria and then Queensland is still one country. Only your first visit adds Australia to your country count.
