The easiest countries to visit in one trip are the ones that sit close together and share open borders: Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg (Benelux), the three Baltic states, or Austria, Slovenia and Croatia. Inside Europe's Schengen Area you can cross between 29 countries without routine passport checks, so a single trip can add three, four or even five countries to your count.

This guide covers six multi-country routes that work well, what to check before you go, and how to count each stop on your map.

## Why is Europe the easiest place to visit several countries in one trip?

Because of the Schengen Area. According to the [European Commission](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/schengen-area_en), it has 29 countries: 25 EU member states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and Liechtenstein. Bulgaria and Romania were the latest to join, with their land border controls lifted on 1 January 2025, as [Wikipedia's Schengen Area article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Area) describes.

Inside the area you can usually travel from one country to the next by train, bus or car without a border stop. Rules can change and temporary checks do happen, so always confirm current entry rules for your passport.

Small neighbors help too. Monaco is treated as part of Schengen because France runs its border controls, and Andorra, San Marino and Vatican City have open or semi-open borders with it, per the same Wikipedia article.

## Route 1: Which route covers three countries fastest, Benelux?

Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg together cover about 75,000 km², according to [Wikipedia's Benelux article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux), a small area for three countries. The three started the cooperation that led to the Schengen process, and cross-border commuting is routine.

A simple plan is to fly into one capital and out of another, and travel overland in between. You might spend a few days in the Netherlands, a few in Belgium, and a day or two in Luxembourg.

## Route 2: Can you visit all three Baltic states in one trip?

Yes. Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, with the capitals Tallinn, Riga and Vilnius, are neighbors in a row, and all three joined the EU on 1 May 2004, according to [Wikipedia's Baltic states article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states). Travelers usually go in one direction between the capitals, by bus, train or car, and fly home from the last one.

Allow roughly a week for a relaxed pace: a few days per capital plus a day trip or two. See the pages for [Estonia](/countries/estonia), [Latvia](/countries/latvia) and [Lithuania](/countries/lithuania).

## Route 3: What about Austria, Slovenia and Croatia?

This is a classic route from the Alps to the Adriatic: Austria, Slovenia and Croatia are all Schengen members, and they border each other in a chain. It suits a road trip, because you cross borders quickly and the scenery changes from mountains to coast.

Start in [Austria](/countries/austria), continue to [Slovenia](/countries/slovenia), and end on the coast in [Croatia](/countries/croatia). If you add Italy or Hungary, you have a five-country trip.

## Route 4: Which route adds the tiny countries?

Europe's microstates are the cheapest extra countries you can find, because each is surrounded by a larger neighbor.

- **Liechtenstein** sits between Switzerland and Austria, so it combines with the Alps route above. See the [Liechtenstein page](/countries/liechtenstein).
- **Monaco** is an easy day trip on the French Riviera: [Monaco](/countries/monaco).
- **San Marino** and **Vatican City** are enclosed by Italy, so a trip through Italy can add both: [San Marino](/countries/san-marino) and [Vatican City](/countries/vatican-city).

Our guide to the [smallest countries in the world](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world) lists the rest, and [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) helps you decide whether a quick stop is enough.

## Route 5: What about Switzerland, Liechtenstein and Austria?

Switzerland, Liechtenstein and Austria are neighbors, and all three are Schengen members, so this is a compact Alpine loop. Liechtenstein sits between the other two, which makes it the natural middle stop. Start in [Switzerland](/countries/switzerland), cross to Liechtenstein, and finish in Austria, or run the chain in reverse.

## Route 6: What should you know about the Balkans?

The Balkans make a rewarding overland trip, with several countries close together: Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia and Kosovo all appear in Tripfolio's country list. The key difference from the routes above is that these six are not Schengen members, so expect border checks on each crossing and check each country's entry rules for your passport. Allow more time than the map suggests, since border queues and slower roads add up.

## How do you plan a multi-country trip?

1. **Pick countries that touch.** Every extra border adds time, so a chain of neighbors beats a list of favorites.
2. **Check entry rules for each country.** For visa-free visits, short stays in the Schengen Area are limited to 90 days within any 180-day period, per the European Commission, and those days are shared across all member countries rather than counted per country.
3. **Fly open-jaw.** Arrive in one city and leave from another so you never backtrack.
4. **Use trains and buses between close capitals.** It saves airport time and usually lets you see more.
5. **Plan at least two nights per stop.** Otherwise you spend the trip in transit.

## How do you count each country on your map?

Each sovereign country you visit counts once, no matter how many times you cross a border. ## FAQ

### How many countries can you visit in one trip?

As many as your time allows. In Europe, the Benelux countries or the three Baltic states give you three countries in about a week, and microstates can add more at almost no extra cost.

### Which countries are best to visit together?

Neighbors with open borders: Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg; Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania; or Austria, Slovenia and Croatia.

### Do I need a passport check between Schengen countries?

Routine checks are not carried out at internal borders in the Schengen Area, but temporary checks can be introduced, so always carry your passport or ID.

### Does a short stop count as visiting a country?

That is your call, and there is no official rule. Many travelers count a country once they have spent real time there, not just changed trains. Our guide on [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) covers the common views.
