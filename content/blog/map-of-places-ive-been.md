The fastest way to make a **map of places I've been** is to use a web map where you tap the countries you've visited and it colors them in for you. If you want city pins instead, a custom map like Google My Maps works better, and if you like spreadsheets, a free geo chart can shade countries from a simple list. Which one is right depends on one question: do you want to show countries and regions, or exact places?

This guide walks through five ways to build your own map, what each one is good and bad at, and how to decide. It also covers the step most people skip: deciding what counts as "been" before you start, so your map doesn't change every time you look at it.

## What kind of map of places you've been do you want?

There are two different maps hiding behind the same search, and mixing them up is why many people give up halfway.

- **A coverage map** colors whole countries, states or provinces. It answers "how much of the world have I seen?" and gives you a number, such as 23 countries.
- **A pin map** marks exact spots: a city, a hotel, a mountain hut, a restaurant. It answers "where exactly did I go?" and looks like a scrapbook.

Coverage maps are quick. You can finish one in ten minutes from memory. Pin maps are slow but personal, because every pin can carry a name, a note and a photo. Many travelers end up with both: a coverage map for the big picture and pins for the trips they love most.

## Way 1: Color it in on a web map

A web map lists countries for you, so you tap the ones you've visited and it fills them in and counts. There's nothing to design and nothing to install.

What to look for:

- **A stated list.** Many travelers count against 195, which is the UN's [193 member states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states) plus the [two non-member observer states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/non-member-states), the Holy See and the State of Palestine. Our guide to [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world) explains why other lists give other totals.
- **Easy undo.** Tap again to remove a country you marked by mistake.
- **A wishlist color.** The blank space is half the fun.
- **Sharing.** A link or image your friends can open without signing up.

On Tripfolio, the [countries visited map](/countries-visited-map?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=map-of-places-ive-been) works this way: tap countries and US states, see your count and percentage, and make a share card without an account. Visited countries are green and wishlist countries are orange. Territories are colored on the map but are not counted toward the 195.

**Best for:** a quick, shareable picture of countries. **Weak at:** exact places inside a city.

## Way 2: Build a pin map with Google My Maps

Google My Maps is the tool for exact places. You make a custom map, name it, and add markers by searching for a place or clicking the spot, as described in Google's help pages on [creating a map](https://support.google.com/mymaps/answer/3024454) and [adding places](https://support.google.com/mymaps/answer/3024925).

Some useful limits from Google's documentation: a map holds up to 10,000 lines, shapes or places combined, and each layer holds up to 2,000 of them. Layers are how you keep things tidy. You could make one layer per year, or one per type of place.

Tips for a pin map that stays readable:

1. **Use layers from the start.** For example "Cities", "Hikes" and "Wishlist".
2. **Name pins with the year.** "Lisbon 2023" is easier to scan than "Lisbon".
3. **Add one line of note per pin.** Future you will thank you.
4. **Don't pin everything.** Pin the places you'd recommend, not every train station.

**Best for:** cities, hikes and favorite spots, with notes. **Weak at:** counting countries and showing a clean "x of 195" number, since it's a general map tool, not a country tracker.

## Way 3: Shade countries from a spreadsheet

If you like data, a spreadsheet can draw a coverage map. Google's chart tools include a geo chart that colors regions from a list. According to Google's [Geochart documentation](https://developers.google.com/chart/interactive/docs/gallery/geochart), it accepts country names or ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes such as "GB", and ISO 3166-2 codes such as "US-NJ" for regions, and you can mix formats across rows. Regions with a value get shaded on a color scale, and regions with no data get a default light color.

The recipe is simple:

1. Make two columns: the country name, and a 1 for visited.
2. Add countries you want to visit with a 0 or a different number, if you want two shades.
3. Insert a geo or map chart and point it at the two columns.

The upside is full control, and the sheet doubles as your list. The downside is the setup time, odd chart defaults, and the fact that sharing means sharing a chart, not a nice card. Spelling also matters: "Czechia", "Czech Republic" and "Türkiye" or "Turkey" can trip up a chart.

**Best for:** people who already live in spreadsheets. **Weak at:** looks, sharing and phones.

## Way 4: Buy a scratch-off or pin poster

A physical world map on the wall is a lovely object. You scratch off countries or push in pins, and guests ask about it. It is also permanent: you can't undo a scratch, and a poster won't tell you your percentage or your count by continent.

We compared the two styles in our [scratch map alternative guide](/blog/scratch-map-alternative). The short version: a poster is a decoration and a conversation starter, a digital map is a record you can fix, search and share.

**Best for:** a gift or a wall. **Weak at:** corrections, regions, and anything beyond the countries printed on the poster.

## Way 5: Use a travel app that keeps trips behind the map

If you want the map to be an index to your trips, choose a tool where each colored country opens a list of trips, dates and photos. That turns "I've been to Japan" into "Japan: April 2024, ten days, Kyoto and Osaka". Our [travel map app checklist](/blog/travel-map-app) lists eight questions to ask before you commit.

On Tripfolio, each country has a sheet with your status, your trips there and friends who've been or want to go. Trips can hold dates, cities, a story and photos. If you can't remember which year you went, the country sheet offers one-tap year chips, and Import from photos can suggest countries from the location data in your photos, read on your device. Photos without location data are skipped.

**Best for:** people who want the map and the memories in one place. **Weak at:** pin-level detail, such as marking a single restaurant.

## How do you decide what counts as "been"?

Before you enter anything, pick a rule. Do airport layovers count? A cruise stop? A day trip across a border? There is no official answer, and the most common mistake is changing your rule halfway and ending up with a number you can't explain.

Write one sentence, such as "I count a country if I left the airport or crossed on land," and stick to it. Our guide to [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) compares the common rules.

The same goes for regions. If you road trip, a map that shows the USA as one block hides most of your travel. On Tripfolio the USA is split into 50 states with an "x / 50 states" counter, and Canada and Australia are split into provinces, territories and states. See the guides to the [US states visited map](/blog/us-states-visited-map) and the [Canada provinces visited map](/blog/canada-provinces-visited-map).

## A simple way to fill in your map in ten minutes

Whichever tool you choose, this order works well:

1. **Start with home and the obvious.** Add your own country and the places you know you've been.
2. **Go region by region.** Walk through Europe, then Asia, then the Americas. Memory works better with a prompt.
3. **Check your photos.** Scroll back by year to find the countries you forgot.
4. **Add the wishlist.** Five places is enough to start. A country like [Japan](/countries/japan) is a classic first entry.
5. **Share it.** Send it to one friend and ask where they have been.

## FAQ

### How do I make a map of the places I've been?

Pick a coverage map if you want countries colored in, or a pin map if you want exact places. For countries, a web map where you tap each one is fastest. For cities and hikes, a custom map like Google My Maps works well.

### Is there a free map of places I've visited?

Yes. Google My Maps, a spreadsheet geo chart and Tripfolio's web map are all free to start. Check each one for what you can share and how it counts.

### Can I make a map of places I've been without signing up?

Yes, for the simple version. On Tripfolio's try-it map you can tap countries and make a share card without creating an account. Sign up afterwards if you want to keep it.

### What's the difference between a visited map and a pin map?

A visited map colors whole countries or regions and gives you a count. A pin map marks exact places with notes. Many travelers use both.

### How many countries should my map show?

Most travelers count against 195: the 193 UN member states plus the Holy See and the State of Palestine. Some maps also color territories, which is fine as long as they are kept out of the total.
