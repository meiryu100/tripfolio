The best travel bucket list ideas fall into a few themes: natural wonders, ancient sites, living cities, big journeys and quiet places few people go. Pick one or two places per theme, write them down where you will see them, and rank them by how much you want to go, not by what other people say you should see.

Below are travel bucket list ideas sorted by theme, a method for turning a long wish list into a short one, and a way to keep it all on a map.

## How do you build a travel bucket list that you'll actually use?

Most bucket lists fail for one reason: they're a pile of everything. A list of 200 places feels like homework, so nobody opens it. Three rules fix that:

- **Start with a feeling, not a place.** Ask what you want more of: silence, food, history, wildlife, a mountain you earned. Then look for places that give you that.
- **Keep two tiers.** A "next" tier of 5 to 10 places you could realistically reach in the next two or three years, and a "someday" tier for the rest. Move places up when money, time or a friend with a spare room lines up.
- **Write why next to each one.** "Petra" is a name. "Walk the Siq at dawn before the tour groups" is a plan.

## Which natural wonders belong on a travel bucket list?

Nature is the easiest theme to start with, because the places are unmistakable. Some classics:

- **The Great Barrier Reef, Australia.** The world's largest coral reef system, with more than 2,900 individual reefs and 900 islands, stretching over 2,300 km ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef)). It was added to the World Heritage list in 1981. See [Australia](/countries/australia).
- **The Galápagos Islands, Ecuador.** The Galápagos were World Heritage Site number 1, so they sit at the very start of UNESCO's list ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site)). See [Ecuador](/countries/ecuador).
- **Northern Lights season in Iceland or northern Norway.** A trip built around a sky you can't schedule. See [Iceland](/countries/iceland).
- **Mount Kilimanjaro, Tanzania.** The big walk for people who want a summit without technical climbing. See [Tanzania](/countries/tanzania).
- **Fiordland and the South Island, New Zealand.** Mountains, lakes and sea in one road trip. See [New Zealand](/countries/new-zealand).
- **A national park road trip in the western US.** Canyons, geysers and redwoods, one state at a time.

## Which historic places are worth the trip?

History places are easier to rank, because many of them are officially recognized. UNESCO's World Heritage List had 1,273 sites in 173 countries as of July 2026: 991 cultural, 240 natural and 42 mixed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site)). That's a huge menu, so here are a few famous ones to start from:

- **Machu Picchu, Peru.** Inscribed in 1983. Daily visitor numbers are capped, with a limit of 4,500 raised in 2024, so plan around that rather than hoping to walk in ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machu_Picchu)). See [Peru](/countries/peru).
- **Petra, Jordan.** Inscribed in 1985 and voted one of the New 7 Wonders of the World in a 2007 popular poll ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra)). See [Jordan](/countries/jordan).
- **Angkor, Cambodia.** A whole landscape of temples that rewards three days more than one.
- **Cappadocia, Türkiye.** Rock-cut churches, valleys and the famous sunrise skyline.
- **Kyoto and the temples around it, Japan.** Best done slowly, with one neighborhood a day. See [Japan](/countries/japan).
- **Rome, Athens or Istanbul.** Three cities where almost every street corner is older than your country.

Remember that a longer list isn't a better list. Ten sites you give three days each are worth more than thirty you tick off in a weekend each.

## What are good bucket list ideas for city lovers?

Not every dream is a mountain. If food, music and people-watching are your thing:

- **Tokyo for a week.** Neighborhoods, trains, tiny restaurants, convenience stores that put some hotels to shame.
- **Lisbon and Porto by train.** Hills, tiles and custard tarts.
- **Mexico City.** Markets, museums and street food with a real sense of scale.
- **Marrakech.** The medina, the rooftops, the tea. Go with patience and a good map.
- **Buenos Aires.** Late dinners, bookshops, tango halls.
- **Istanbul.** A city on two continents, where a ferry ride is a good afternoon.

## Which big journeys make the best bucket list items?

Some bucket list ideas are about the route, not the place:

- **A long train journey.** A night train across a country counts as a destination.
- **A cross-country road trip.** A drive across the US, Canada or Australia teaches you how big a country really is.
- **A month in one place.** Not a trip so much as a different way of living for a while.
- **Every country in one region.** For example, all the countries of South America or the Caribbean. Our guides to [how many countries are in South America](/blog/how-many-countries-in-south-america) and [the Caribbean](/blog/how-many-countries-in-the-caribbean) are a good start for a checklist.
- **All 50 US states, or every Canadian province.** A finite, rewarding goal. Track it with the [US states visited map](/us-states-visited-map).

## What are quiet, off-the-beaten-path ideas?

Every list needs a few wild cards, so it isn't only the famous names. Instead of copying someone else's list, find your own:

- **Look at the smallest countries.** Our list of the [smallest countries in the world](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world) is full of places you can see in a day.
- **Look at a map for the gray gaps.** The region you know least about is often the one worth exploring next.
- **Ask a friend where they went last.** The best tips come from someone you trust, with photos.
- **Choose a country because you can't picture it.** If you can't imagine what a day there looks like, that's the point.

## How do you choose between all your options?

Once your list has grown long, you need a way to choose. Try this:

1. **Score each place from 1 to 5** on three questions: how much do I want it, how long will it be available the way I imagine it, and how realistic is it in the next three years.
2. **Add the three scores.** The top ten become your "next" tier.
3. **Check the season.** Some places have a window (northern lights, a Kilimanjaro dry season, a monsoon). Note the best months next to each.
4. **Look for pairs.** Two places that are close together are often one trip, not two. Peru and Ecuador, for example, are neighbors, so one long flight can cover both.
5. **Review once a year.** Tastes change. It's fine to delete a place you no longer care about.

## FAQ

### What should be on a travel bucket list?

A mix of natural wonders, historic sites, cities, big journeys and a few places few people visit. Choose by what you want to feel, such as awe, curiosity or rest, rather than by what is popular.

### How many places should a travel bucket list have?

Enough to excite you, but not so many that you never open it. A "next" list of 5 to 10 places and a longer "someday" list works well for most people.

### How do I choose between my bucket list destinations?

Score each place on how much you want it, how long it will stay as you imagine, and how realistic it is soon. Add the scores, take the top ten, and group places that are near each other into one trip.

### Should a bucket list include the places I've already been?

Many travelers keep both: a map of where they've been and a wishlist of where they want to go. Seeing the two together shows the gaps and makes the next trip easier to pick.

Yes. Tripfolio is free and runs in your browser. You can mark countries as visited or want to visit, and the wishlist groups them by continent.
