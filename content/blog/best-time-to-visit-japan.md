The best time to visit Japan for most travelers is spring (late March to early May) and autumn (roughly September to November), when the weather is mild and the cherry blossoms or fall colors are out. But "best" depends on what you want: snow, festivals, fewer crowds or lower prices all point to different months.

Here is how each season works, which dates to avoid, and how to choose, based on what the official calendars and climate records say.

## When is the best time to visit Japan?

If you want one rule: pick the season first, then the region. Japan stretches from snowy Hokkaido in the north to the subtropical Ryukyu Islands in the south, and the climate differs a lot between them. Wikipedia's [Climate of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Japan) article describes six main climate zones, including a humid continental climate in Hokkaido, heavy winter snow on the Sea of Japan side, and a milder, sunnier winter on the Pacific side.

A quick way to choose:

- **Cherry blossoms:** late March to early May, depending on where you are.
- **Mild, comfortable weather:** spring and autumn.
- **Snow and winter sports:** winter, mostly on the Sea of Japan side and in Hokkaido.
- **Beaches in the south:** summer, but mind the typhoon months.
- **Fewer crowds:** avoid Golden Week, mid-August and the New Year break (details below).

## What is cherry blossom season in Japan?

Cherry trees bloom across Japan from late March to early May. The bloom moves from south to north, and in Okinawa the first blossoms come as early as January, according to Wikipedia's [Hanami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanami) article. Blossoms usually last one to two weeks, so timing is the hard part.

A few things to know:

- **Dates shift every year.** As an example from the same article, in 2018 Fukuoka's trees opened on March 21, Tokyo on March 26 and Kyoto on March 27, while Sapporo in Hokkaido was scheduled for May 1. Treat those as one year's example, not a promise.
- **Forecasts come late.** Exact bloom forecasts are published by the Japan Meteorological Agency and private weather companies shortly before the season, so you cannot know your exact dates when you book. Pick a window of a week or two on either side, and be ready to move around the country if the bloom comes early or late.
- **Going north extends the season.** If you miss the bloom in Tokyo or Kyoto, blossoms are still opening later in the north.

Spring is also popular, so book flights and rooms early.

## What are Golden Week and the other holiday rush periods?

Japan has 16 national holidays, listed on Wikipedia's [Public holidays in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Japan) page. A few of them bunch together into the busiest travel periods for people who live in Japan:

- **Golden Week (29 April to 5 May).** It includes Showa Day (29 April), Constitution Memorial Day (3 May), Greenery Day (4 May) and Children's Day (5 May). Wikipedia notes that Golden Week "often sees a surge in vacation travel throughout the country" ([Golden Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Week_(Japan))). Expect full trains and busy sights.
- **Mid-August.** Mountain Day (11 August) was set to coincide with the mid-August Bon festival vacation period, when many people travel to their home towns.
- **New Year (about 29 December to 3 January).** Not a legal requirement, but many workplaces close then, and some close from Christmas Eve to 5 January. Some shops and restaurants may close too, so check before you plan a trip around it.

None of these dates are impossible. They are just busier, so book early or choose the days around them.

## Is June a bad time to visit because of the rainy season?

Not necessarily. The rainy season (tsuyu) in Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu generally runs from mid-June to mid-July, according to the climate article above. Two exceptions help you plan:

- **Hokkaido has no rainy season,** because the seasonal rain front fades in northern Honshu before reaching it.
- **Okinawa's rainy season is earlier:** from early May to mid-June, and it rains less often and less continuously than on the mainland.

So June can suit you if you head to Hokkaido, and the rainy season brings fewer crowds in many places. Pack a light rain jacket and plan indoor options.

## What is summer like, and when is typhoon season?

Summer is hot and humid. In Tokyo, the 1991–2020 climate normals show a mean daily maximum of 29.9 °C in July and 31.3 °C in August, with daily minimums above 22 °C. Japan's highest recorded temperature is 41.8 °C, set in Isesaki on 5 August 2025.

Typhoons are the other factor. Five or six typhoons pass over or near Japan each year from early August to early October, per the same source, though typhoons can strike anywhere between July and October. If you travel in that window, keep your schedule flexible and buy travel insurance that covers weather cancellations.

Summer works if you want mountains, Hokkaido, festivals or the southern islands. If you are not good with heat and humidity, choose another season for city sightseeing.

## When can you see autumn colors?

Leaf-peeping, called momijigari in Japanese, is the autumn counterpart to cherry blossoms. Wikipedia places the peak of autumn color in the Northern Hemisphere "around September to November" ([Autumn leaf color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn_leaf_color)), and lists Japan among the regions with the brightest displays. I could not find a reliable official source with exact dates by city, so check a current foliage forecast close to your trip rather than trusting a fixed date in any guide. As with the cherry blossoms, the best days are short and the timing shifts from year to year.

## When is the best time to visit Japan for snow and winter?

Winter snow is heaviest on the Sea of Japan side, especially in Hokkaido and the mountains of northern and central Honshu. Sapporo and Asahikawa get snowfall totals of hundreds of inches a season. The Pacific side, including Tokyo, is far milder and sunnier. Tokyo's average snowfall is only a few centimeters, falling from December to February.

That makes winter a good choice if you want:

- **Skiing and snow scenery** in Hokkaido or the Japanese Alps.
- **Clear, dry days in Tokyo** and the Pacific coast.
- **Hot springs** in cold weather.

Just plan around the New Year closures mentioned above.

## So which month should you pick?

Use this short list as a starting point:

1. **Late March to early April:** cherry blossoms in the central cities. Popular and busy.
2. **Late April to early May:** blossoms in the north, but Golden Week crowds in the rest of the country.
3. **June:** rainy season in most of the country, but fine in Hokkaido.
4. **July and August:** hot and humid, festival season, typhoons possible from August.
5. **September to November:** mild weather and autumn color, with typhoons possible early on.
6. **December to February:** snow in the north and west, dry and clear in Tokyo, closures at New Year.

A good compromise is late October to mid-November or early-to-mid April. Both are mild, but check the year's forecasts before you book anything non-refundable.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest time to visit Japan?

There is no single cheapest month, but prices usually rise in cherry blossom season, Golden Week, mid-August and around New Year. Going in the rainy season, in early summer or outside those peaks usually gives you a better chance at lower prices. Check current fares and room rates for your own dates.

### Is it worth visiting Japan in the rainy season?

Yes, if you accept some wet days. The rainy season in Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu generally runs from mid-June to mid-July, and Hokkaido has none. Okinawa's rainy season comes earlier, from early May to mid-June.

### When do the cherry blossoms bloom in Tokyo and Kyoto?

Usually in late March to early April, but the exact dates change each year, and blossoms last only one to two weeks. In 2018, for example, Tokyo's bloom opened on 26 March and Kyoto's on 27 March. Check a current forecast before you book.

### When is typhoon season in Japan?

Five or six typhoons pass over or near Japan each year from early August to early October, and they can occur between July and October. Keep your plans flexible in that window.
