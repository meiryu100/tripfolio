The **G20 countries list** has 19 members: Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Türkiye, the United Kingdom and the United States. Two organizations sit at the table too, the European Union and, since 2023, the African Union, which is why you'll see the G20 described as having 21 members. Below is the full list, how the group started, who attends without being a member, and how the 19 countries spread across the continents.

## Which countries are in the G20?

The [Bundesbank's G20 page](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/tasks/topics/g20-the-group-of-twenty-645740) names the 19 countries plus the European Union, and [Wikipedia's G20 article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20) lists the African Union as the 21st member. In alphabetical order, the countries are:

1. Argentina
2. Australia
3. Brazil
4. Canada
5. China
6. France
7. Germany
8. India
9. Indonesia
10. Italy
11. Japan
12. Mexico
13. Russia
14. Saudi Arabia
15. South Africa
16. South Korea
17. Türkiye (listed as Turkey in many sources)
18. United Kingdom
19. United States

The "20" in the name counts the 19 countries plus the European Union. The African Union was added later, so the number in the name no longer matches the number of members. It is a name that stuck, not a headcount.

## Why is it called the G20 if there are 21 members?

The group was formally established on 26 September 1999 at a meeting of G7 finance ministers, according to Wikipedia, with the first meeting held in Berlin on 15 and 16 December 1999. The Bundesbank describes the reason: after the Asian crisis of the late 1990s, and with emerging economies becoming more important to the world economy, the G7 agreed to create a place for dialogue between industrial and emerging market countries.

Leaders began meeting at an annual summit in 2008, after the global financial crisis. Wikipedia describes the 2008 Washington gathering as the first summit. The African Union joined as the 21st member at the 2023 New Delhi summit, and it was officially represented at the 2024 meeting in Rio de Janeiro.

## Who runs the G20?

There is no permanent headquarters. The Bundesbank explains that members take turns chairing the group each year, and that the past, present and future chairs work together as the "G20 Troika" so that handovers go smoothly. According to Wikipedia, the United States holds the chair in 2026 and the summit is planned for Miami.

The International Monetary Fund and the World Bank are described by the Bundesbank as ex officio members.

## Who is invited without being a member?

Some countries attend regularly but are not on the list. Per Wikipedia:

- **Spain** is a permanent guest.
- **The chair of ASEAN** and a NEPAD representative are also permanent guests.
- **The host country** chooses other invitees, usually one or two countries from its own region.
- International bodies such as the IMF, OECD, United Nations, World Bank and WTO are invited to take part in their own policy areas.

The Netherlands also attends as a guest rather than a member, according to the same article. So if you see a photo of a G20 summit with more than 19 flags, that's why.

## How were the members chosen?

Wikipedia says there are no formal criteria for membership, and the list doesn't exactly match the 20 or 21 largest economies. It quotes the political economist Robert Wade as saying that Portugal, Nigeria and Egypt were left out of the original 1999 selection. In practice, the members are the G7 states plus a dozen other systemically important countries, as the Bundesbank puts it.

That's worth remembering if you're using the list as a "biggest countries" shortcut. It is a political list, not a ranking. For lists that do follow a measurable rule, see our guide to the [largest countries in the world by area](/blog/largest-countries-in-the-world).

## How much of the world is in the G20?

The sources disagree on the exact numbers, and neither page gives a date for its figures, so treat them as rough:

- The Bundesbank says the G20 covers about two-thirds of the world's population, almost 90% of gross world product and roughly 80% of world trade.
- Wikipedia says the 19 member states alone account for around 85% of gross world product, 75% of international trade and 56% of the world's population. With the EU and AU included, it gives 78.9% of the global population.

The gap comes from what is counted. The Wikipedia figures are for the 19 states, while including the EU and AU brings in many more countries. If you quote a number, say which source it comes from.

## Which continents do the G20 countries cover?

Here is a view only a map gives you. Using the continents in the country list behind Tripfolio, the 19 members split like this:

- **Asia (7):** China, India, Indonesia, Japan, Saudi Arabia, South Korea and Türkiye
- **Europe (5):** France, Germany, Italy, Russia and the United Kingdom
- **North America (3):** Canada, Mexico and the United States
- **South America (2):** Argentina and Brazil
- **Africa (1):** South Africa
- **Oceania (1):** Australia

That adds up to 19. Antarctica has no countries. Two choices here are worth flagging. Tripfolio files Türkiye under Asia, and Russia under Europe, even though both span two continents. Other lists may group them differently. See [how many continents there are](/blog/how-many-continents-are-there) for why that question has several correct answers.

The result is lopsided. Africa has 54 sovereign countries on our map, and only South Africa is a G20 member in its own right.

## FAQ

### How many countries are in the G20?

Nineteen countries. With the European Union and the African Union, the group has 21 members.

### Which countries are in the G20?

Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Türkiye, the United Kingdom and the United States.

### Is Spain a member of the G20?

No. Spain attends every summit as a permanent guest, but it is not a member. The same goes for the Netherlands, which attends as a guest.

### When did the African Union join the G20?

The African Union became the 21st member at the 2023 summit in New Delhi.

### Who holds the G20 presidency in 2026?

The United States, according to Wikipedia, with the 2026 summit planned for Miami. The presidency rotates every year.
