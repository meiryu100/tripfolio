The most common travel milestones are **1, 10, 25, 50 and 100 countries visited**. There is no official ladder, so these are traveler tradition. Each step is roughly a different kind of traveler: 10 is a habit, 25 is a pattern, 50 is a lifestyle and 100 is a long project that usually takes decades.

This guide explains what each of those travel milestones means, how the count changes depending on your rules, and how to keep track so the numbers are honest.

## What are the travel milestones for countries visited?

Travelers have settled on a handful of round numbers. Tripfolio uses the same ladder on its map: your first country, then 10, 25, 50 and 100. Some trackers add 5, 75, 150 or "everything", but the five above are the ones most people talk about.

- **1 country:** your first stamp, or your first border if you grew up in a big country.
- **10 countries:** you travel on purpose.
- **25 countries:** you have a taste and a pattern, and probably a continent you keep going back to.
- **50 countries:** you have seen a quarter of the world's sovereign states.
- **100 countries:** you are in a small club, and you have planned for it.

None of this is a competition. The numbers are just handy checkpoints, and a person with 12 countries who knows each one deeply has done something a 60-country box-ticker has not.

## How much of the world is each milestone?

Percentages help put the numbers in perspective. The UN has [193 member states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states). Tripfolio counts **195 sovereign countries**, which is the 193 UN members plus the Holy See (Vatican City) and Palestine, the two UN observer states. We explain the different totals in [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world).

Using Tripfolio's 195 as the base:

- 10 countries is about 5% of the world.
- 25 countries is about 13%.
- 50 countries is about 26%, roughly a quarter.
- 100 countries is about 51%, just over half.

So when someone says they have "seen half the world", 100 is the number. It also shows how big the last half is. Getting from 100 to 195 means visiting every remote island nation, every landlocked state and every country that is hard to reach.

## What does each milestone look like in practice?

### 10 countries: the habit

Ten is reachable surprisingly fast if you live in a region with short borders. A European can pass 10 with a few long weekends and one summer rail trip. Someone living in North America or Australia usually needs a few more flights.

At this stage your map is mostly one cluster. The interesting question is what the next trip will add: a new continent, or just the neighbor you have not yet seen.

### 25 countries: the pattern

By 25 you probably have a favorite region and at least two or three continents. Your map has a recognizable shape. This is also the point where counting rules start to matter, because layovers, border crossings and cruise stops can each add or remove a few countries. We cover that in [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country).

### 50 countries: the lifestyle

Fifty countries usually means travel is built into your calendar. You have probably been on four or five continents, and some of your entries are the small ones that are easy to skip, like Andorra, Liechtenstein or Malta. If you want ideas for cheap additions, see [the smallest countries in the world](/blog/smallest-countries-in-the-world).

### 100 countries: the long project

A hundred is a big number whichever way you count. The [Travelers' Century Club](https://www.travelerscenturyclub.org/), founded in 1954, is built around exactly this number: it is a club for people who have visited 100 or more of the world's countries and territories. Its list is longer than the UN list. According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelers%27_Century_Club), it had 330 entries as of January 2022, because it counts territories and island groups as separate places.

That is the main thing to remember about the 100 milestone. **The same trip history can give you 100 on one list and 85 on another**, depending on whether territories count.

## Which counting rules change your milestone?

Three choices decide your number more than any trip does.

1. **Sovereign countries only, or territories too.** Tripfolio's counter, percentage and milestones use only the 195 sovereign countries. Territories such as Puerto Rico, Greenland or Hong Kong can still be colored on the map, but they do not count toward "x / 195". If you prefer the Travelers' Century Club style, count them yourself and treat the Tripfolio number as the stricter one.
2. **What counts as a visit.** Some people count any time on the ground, others require a night, a meal or leaving the airport. Pick a rule once and keep it. A consistent rule matters more than a strict one.
3. **Regions inside big countries.** Visiting 30 US states does not add to your country count. It is a separate milestone with its own map. Tripfolio splits the USA into 50 states, Canada into 10 provinces and 3 territories, and Australia into 6 states and 2 territories, each with its own counter that sits beside the country total. See [the US states map](/blog/us-states-visited-map) if that is the number you are chasing.

## How do you keep track of your milestones?

Memory is a poor tracker. Most people who try to list their countries from scratch forget two or three, especially short stops from years ago. A better routine:

1. **Start with a list.** Write down every country you remember, then go back through old photos and passports to find the ones you missed.
2. **Choose your rules.** Decide on sovereign-only or territories too, and what counts as a visit.
3. **Put it on a map.** Seeing the gaps is more motivating than a number. A color-coded world map also shows which continents are lagging.
4. **Add a wishlist.** The next milestone is much easier to reach with a short list of places you actually want to visit.

Tripfolio does this on a free web map that works on phones. If you have photos, the Import from photos option reads the countries from your photos' location data on your device, which is a quick way to find the ones you forgot. ## How fast do people usually reach each milestone?

There is no reliable public statistic for how long it takes the average traveler to reach 50 or 100 countries, so we will not pretend there is. What is easy to see is why it varies. Where you live matters: from the middle of Europe, a dozen countries are a day's drive away. How you travel matters too. Ten weeks of slow travel in one region gives a very different count than ten weekends in ten places.

A more useful question than "how fast?" is "what comes next?". The jump from 10 to 25 is usually about adding a new continent. The jump from 25 to 50 often comes from small countries and from choosing neighbors you have skipped. The jump from 50 to 100 usually means long trips, and at least a few places that take real planning, such as the more remote island states.

## Should you care about the numbers at all?

Some travelers have given up counting entirely, and that is a fair choice. The count is a game, not a measure of how well you travel. It works best as a nudge: a number that makes you notice that you have never been to South America, or that you are three countries short of your next milestone and there are three on your wishlist within a short flight.

The best use of a milestone is to celebrate it and then ask what you want from the next one. Plenty of people stop caring about the number at 50 and start caring about depth. Others become completionists and go for all 195.

## FAQ

### How many countries is a lot to have visited?

There is no official threshold. Reaching 25 means you have traveled widely, and 50 puts you well into enthusiast territory. By the Travelers' Century Club's measure, 100 countries and territories is the point where you qualify for membership.

### What is the 100 countries club?

The best-known one is the Travelers' Century Club, founded in 1954 in California. It is open to people who have visited 100 or more of the world's countries and territories, using its own list of 330 places (as of January 2022).

### Do territories count toward travel milestones?

It depends on the list. The Travelers' Century Club counts territories as separate places. Tripfolio counts only the 195 sovereign countries toward its total and milestones, and colors territories on the map without counting them.

### How many countries are there to visit in total?

The UN has 193 member states. Tripfolio counts 195 by adding the two UN observer states, the Holy See and Palestine. Other lists, such as the Travelers' Century Club's, are longer because they include territories.

### Does visiting US states or Canadian provinces count as countries?

No. States and provinces are regions inside a country, so they only add to that country once. They have their own counters, for example "x / 50 states" on Tripfolio's USA map.
