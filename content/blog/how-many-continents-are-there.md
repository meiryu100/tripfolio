There are **seven continents** in the model taught in most English-speaking countries: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia (often called Oceania). That is the answer you want for a quiz, a school form or a travel count. But there is no single official rule, and other parts of the world teach six, five or even four.

This guide lists the seven, shows the other models and why they exist, explains the borderline cases (Europe and Asia, Oceania, Antarctica, Zealandia) and shows how to count the continents you have visited.

## What are the 7 continents?

Here they are, largest to smallest, with area and population from [Wikipedia's Continent article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent). Areas follow Encyclopædia Britannica, and population is a UN estimate for 2021.

1. **Asia**: about 44.6 million km², roughly 4.7 billion people
2. **Africa**: about 30.4 million km², roughly 1.4 billion people
3. **North America**: about 24.2 million km², roughly 596 million people
4. **South America**: about 17.8 million km², roughly 434 million people
5. **Antarctica**: about 14.2 million km², no permanent population
6. **Europe**: about 10 million km², roughly 745 million people
7. **Oceania** (or Australia): about 8.5 million km², roughly 44 million people

Asia is both the largest and the most populated, with almost 60 percent of the world's people. Oceania is the smallest by both measures. Antarctica is bigger than Europe and Oceania, yet nobody lives there year-round.

## Why do some people say there are 6 continents?

Because "continent" is a convention, not a measurement. Wikipedia lists several models in use, each taught in different places:

- **Seven continents**: the model above. It is taught in Australia, Canada, the UK and the US, and also in countries such as China, India, Indonesia and Pakistan.
- **Six, with a combined America**: North and South America count as one continent. It is taught in Greece, in many Romance-speaking countries and across Latin America. Wikipedia says the Olympic rings reflect this model.
- **Six, with Eurasia**: Europe and Asia are merged. It is mostly used in Russia and parts of Eastern Europe.
- **Five**: Africa, Eurasia, America, Antarctica and Australia/Oceania.
- **Four**: Afro-Eurasia, America, Antarctica and Australia/Oceania.

[Britannica's entry on continents](https://www.britannica.com/science/continent) also gives seven as the usual count and mentions that Europe and Asia are sometimes treated as a single continent, Eurasia. So if someone tells you "there are six," they are probably not wrong. They learned a different model.

## Where does Europe end and Asia begin?

There is no natural boundary. Europe and Asia sit on one landmass, and the line between them is a human choice. The ancient Greeks used the Black Sea and the Bosporus. Later maps ran the line along the Rioni River in Georgia, or through the Kerch Strait and along the Don River in Russia.

This matters when you count, because a few countries straddle the line. Using the UN's geographic groupings, Wikipedia notes that all of Russia is placed in Europe, while Türkiye and Kazakhstan are placed in Asia. We follow our own country-by-country logic in the guides to [how many countries are in Europe](/blog/how-many-countries-in-europe) and [how many countries are in Asia](/blog/how-many-countries-in-asia). On Tripfolio's map, Russia is in Europe, Türkiye and Kazakhstan are in Asia, Georgia is in Asia, and Cyprus is filed under Europe.

## Is Australia a continent, or is it Oceania?

Both words show up, and they mean slightly different things. Mainland Australia is the smallest continental landmass, at about 7.6 million km². **Oceania** is a wider region that takes in Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and most of the Pacific island nations. Many seven-continent lists use "Oceania" so that the Pacific countries have a home, and that is how the 8.5 million km² figure above is counted.

If you are tracking trips, "Oceania" is the more useful label. Fiji, Samoa and New Zealand are not on the Australian landmass, but they all belong to the same continent bucket. The [Australia page](/countries/australia) and [New Zealand page](/countries/new-zealand) show where each sits.

## Is Antarctica a continent?

Yes, in every model above. Charles Wilkes described it as a continent in 1838, which makes it the last one to be identified. It covers about 14.2 million km² and has no permanent population, only research station staff who rotate through.

Antarctica has no countries. The Antarctic Treaty, [signed on 1 December 1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Treaty_System) and in force since 23 June 1961, bans new territorial claims. Seven countries still claim parts of it, and most others do not recognize those claims. That is why Antarctica gives you a continent for your count but no country to add to the 195.

## What about Zealandia, the "eighth continent"?

Zealandia is a mostly underwater mass of continental crust in the Pacific. According to [Wikipedia's Zealandia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealandia), it covers about 4.9 million km², and roughly 94 percent of it is submerged. New Zealand is the largest part above the waves, followed by New Caledonia. In 2017 a team of eleven geologists argued in *GSA Today* that it meets the requirements to be called a submerged continent.

Some geologists still dispute that, and nobody uses Zealandia in school atlases or in travel counts. Think of it as a geology story. A trip to New Zealand still counts as Oceania.

## How many countries are on each continent?

The number of continents is a convention, and so is the number of countries on each. The totals below are the ones Tripfolio's map uses. They count only the 195 sovereign countries, which is also the number we explain in [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world).

- **Asia**: 47
- **Africa**: 54
- **Europe**: 45
- **North America**: 23, including Central America and the Caribbean
- **South America**: 12
- **Oceania**: 14
- **Antarctica**: 0 countries

That adds up to 195. Other sources get slightly different numbers because of the Europe and Asia line. The UN lists 48 countries in Asia, for example, because it places Cyprus there. The continent guides explain each difference: [Africa](/blog/how-many-countries-in-africa), [South America](/blog/how-many-countries-in-south-america) and the others linked above.

## How do you count the continents you've visited?

The simple rule most travelers use: you have been to a continent if you have been to any country on it. It is a different kind of milestone from the country count, and it is a fun one. Seven is the maximum, and Antarctica is the one that takes planning.

Some practical points:

- **Pick a model first.** If you use the seven-continent model, Panama is North America, Egypt is Africa, and Istanbul is split across two continents. A single stop in Istanbul's European side and its Asian side gives you both.
- **Transit rarely counts.** The usual question is what counts as a visit, which we cover in [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country). The same logic applies here.
- **Territories help with the hard ones.** Antarctica has no countries, so a cruise or research-station visit is the only way to tick it.

## FAQ

### How many continents are there in the world?

Seven in the model taught in most English-speaking countries: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia or Oceania. Other regions teach six, five or four.

### Why do some countries teach 6 continents?

Some merge North and South America into one continent called America, which is common in Latin America and Greece. Others merge Europe and Asia into Eurasia, which is common in Russia.

### Which is the largest continent?

Asia, at about 44.6 million km² and roughly 4.7 billion people. Oceania is the smallest, at about 8.5 million km².

### Is Antarctica a continent?

Yes. It covers about 14.2 million km², has no permanent population and has no countries. The Antarctic Treaty has banned new territorial claims since 1961.

### Is Zealandia the eighth continent?

Geologists proposed in 2017 that Zealandia, about 94 percent underwater, is a submerged continent. It is not part of the standard seven, and not everyone agrees with the proposal.
