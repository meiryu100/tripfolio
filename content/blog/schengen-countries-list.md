The Schengen Area has **29 countries**: 25 EU member states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and Liechtenstein. Inside it you cross borders without routine passport checks. The newest members are Bulgaria and Romania, whose land borders opened on 1 January 2025. Here is the full Schengen countries list, the date each country joined, the two EU members that are still outside, and a simple way to keep track of the ones you've visited.

## Which Schengen countries are not in the EU?

Four: **Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and Liechtenstein**. They joined the zone through their own agreements, not through EU membership. This is the part that surprises people. Switzerland is inside Schengen but is not an EU member, so the two lists differ. Our guide to [all 27 countries in the European Union](/blog/countries-in-the-european-union) covers the EU side.

If you're planning a trip to [Switzerland](/countries/switzerland), [Norway](/countries/norway) or [Iceland](/countries/iceland), your Schengen rules apply there even though the EU's other rules (such as the euro) do not.

## When did each country join Schengen?

Schengen began in 1995 with seven states and grew in waves. These dates are the first implementation of border-control abolition, from Wikipedia's summary table:

- **26 March 1995:** Belgium, France, Germany, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal and Spain.
- **26 October 1997:** Italy.
- **1 December 1997:** Austria.
- **1 January 2000:** Greece.
- **25 March 2001:** Denmark, Finland, Sweden, Iceland and Norway.
- **21 December 2007:** Czechia, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Slovakia and Slovenia (air borders followed on 30 March 2008).
- **12 December 2008:** Switzerland.
- **19 December 2011:** Liechtenstein.
- **1 January 2023:** Croatia (air borders from 26 March 2023).
- **2024 and 2025:** Bulgaria and Romania. Air and sea borders opened on 31 March 2024, and land borders on 1 January 2025.

Add those up and you get 7, then 15 by 2001, 24 after the 2007 wave, 26 once Switzerland and Liechtenstein were in, and 29 with Croatia, Bulgaria and Romania.

## Which EU countries are not in Schengen?

Two of the 27 EU members are outside, and for different reasons:

- **Ireland** has an opt-out under the Schengen Protocol and keeps its own visa and border policies, according to the European Commission. It still takes part in the Schengen Information System and some police and judicial cooperation.
- **Cyprus** is committed to joining. The Commission says it takes part in Schengen cooperation, but the Council has not yet lifted its internal border controls. Wikipedia gives target dates that have moved, so check the Commission page for the current status before you rely on it.

For a quick check: if you land in [Ireland](/countries/ireland) or [Cyprus](/countries/cyprus) from another EU country, expect a normal border check even though both are EU members.

## What about Monaco, Andorra, San Marino and the Vatican?

They are not full members. Per Wikipedia:

- **Monaco** is treated as part of the area in practice, because French authorities run its border controls.
- **Andorra, San Marino and Vatican City** keep open or semi-open borders with neighboring Schengen states because of their size, but they are not members.

For a trip, that means you can reach [Monaco](/countries/monaco) from France with no formal border crossing in sight. On a personal map, it still counts as its own country, which is how Tripfolio treats it.

## How long can I stay in the Schengen Area?

Travelers from visa-exempt countries (about 60 nationalities, according to Wikipedia) can stay up to **90 days in any 180-day period**. The days are shared across all 29 countries. Ten days in Spain and twenty in Italy use thirty days of the same allowance, not two separate ones.

Rules for your passport can differ, and the EU has been developing the ETIAS pre-travel authorization for visa-exempt visitors. Wikipedia describes it as still in development, with mandatory use planned from 2027. Check the official EU travel pages for the exact start date before you book.

## Is Schengen the same as the EU or the eurozone?

No. Three lists overlap but are not the same:

- **EU:** 27 countries.
- **Schengen Area:** 29 countries, including four non-EU ones, and without Ireland and Cyprus.
- **Euro:** a currency, not a travel zone. Some Schengen countries, such as Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Czechia, don't use it.

Wider Europe is a fourth, bigger list, covered in [how many countries are in Europe](/blog/how-many-countries-in-europe). Belarus, Ukraine, Albania and the United Kingdom are in Europe but in neither the EU nor Schengen.

## How do I use Schengen to collect countries on your map?

Because borders are open, Schengen is the easiest place to add several countries on one trip. A week by train can take you through [France](/countries/france), Belgium, Luxembourg and Germany. A road trip through the Alps can add Liechtenstein and Switzerland between Austria and Germany. Our post on [countries you can visit in one trip](/blog/countries-you-can-visit-in-one-trip) lays out route ideas.

A few points about counting:

- **A transit stop usually isn't a visit.** See [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) and decide your own rule before you start ticking.
- **Territories don't add to your total.** On Tripfolio only the 195 sovereign countries count toward your "x / 195" and your percentage. Territories can be colored but aren't scored.
- **Microstates do count.** Liechtenstein and Monaco are sovereign countries, so each is its own entry.

## FAQ

### How many countries are in the Schengen Area?

Twenty-nine. That is 25 EU member states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and Liechtenstein, according to the European Commission.

### Which country joined Schengen most recently?

Bulgaria and Romania. Their air and sea borders opened on 31 March 2024, and the land borders on 1 January 2025.

### Is Ireland in the Schengen Area?

No. Ireland is an EU member but has an opt-out under the Schengen Protocol, so it runs its own border and visa policies.

### Is Switzerland in Schengen?

Yes. Switzerland joined on 12 December 2008, even though it is not an EU member.

### How long can I stay in the Schengen Area without a visa?

Citizens of visa-exempt countries can stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period, and the days count across all member countries together. Check the rules for your own passport.
