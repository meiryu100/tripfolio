There are **seven countries in Central America**: Belize, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama. All seven are sovereign. The one list that says eight is the United Nations' geographic grouping, which also files Mexico under Central America. Almost everyone else, from atlases to Wikipedia's [Central America article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America), treats Mexico as part of North America and uses seven.

So the honest answer depends on who is asking. If you're settling a trivia question, say seven. If you're counting countries for a travel list, the question is really "which definition am I using?", and this guide shows you how to pick one and stick to it.

## What are the countries in Central America?

Here is the list from north to south, with capitals:

1. **Belize**: Belmopan
2. **Guatemala**: Guatemala City
3. **Honduras**: Tegucigalpa
4. **El Salvador**: San Salvador
5. **Nicaragua**: Managua
6. **Costa Rica**: San José
7. **Panama**: Panama City

Central America is the narrow land bridge that joins North America to South America. It sits south of Mexico and ends where Panama meets Colombia. The region is small, about 523,780 square kilometers in total according to Wikipedia, so it is one of the easiest parts of the world to travel through several countries in one trip.

## Why do some lists say eight?

The UN Statistics Division publishes a geographic grouping called [M49](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/), used for statistics. Its "Central America" entry (code 013) has **eight** members: the seven above plus **Mexico**.

That doesn't mean the UN thinks Mexico is culturally or politically Central American. The M49 scheme is a statistical convenience: it puts every mainland state south of the United States and north of South America into one bucket. Mexico is historically and politically considered part of North America, which is how most maps, schools and travelers think of it.

For your own count, the difference is one country. Pick the seven-country version unless you have a reason to follow the UN list, and write down which one you chose.

## Is Mexico part of Central America?

Not in everyday usage. Mexico borders Guatemala and Belize, and its southern states (Chiapas, Yucatán, Quintana Roo) share history and food with the region, so the confusion is understandable. But Mexico's capital, language, politics and trade all point north, and Mexico is generally counted as North America.

If you're building a list by continent, Mexico goes with the United States and Canada. You can browse it on its own page: [Mexico](/countries/mexico).

## Is Belize in Central America or the Caribbean?

Both, depending on who you ask. Belize has a Caribbean coast, was a British colony (British Honduras) and became independent on 21 September 1981. Wikipedia notes it is considered part of both Central America and the Caribbean. It is also the only Central American country where English is the official language.

The UN lists Belize in Central America, not in its Caribbean group, and so do we. That's why it doesn't appear in our count of [countries in the Caribbean](/blog/how-many-countries-in-the-caribbean). You can read more on its [Belize country page](/countries/belize).

## What about Panama, Colombia and the Dominican Republic?

A few edge cases come up again and again:

- **Panama** is the southernmost Central American country. It is home to the Panama Canal, which opened on 15 August 1914 and has been under full Panamanian control since 31 December 1999. See the [Panama country page](/countries/panama).
- **Colombia** is in South America, even though it borders Panama. It is part of our [South America count of 12](/blog/how-many-countries-in-south-america).
- **The Dominican Republic** is a Caribbean island nation. It is a full member of the Central American Integration System (SICA), a regional organization, since 27 June 2013, which is why some lists include it. Geographically it is not in Central America.

SICA has eight full members: Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama as founders (the Protocol of Tegucigalpa was signed on 13 December 1991), Belize, which joined in 1998, and the Dominican Republic. Mexico is only an observer. Memberships like this are a political choice, not a map boundary, so they're a poor guide for counting, and they are another reason you'll see the number eight in some places.

This is why "how many countries in Central America" gets different answers online. One source counts the UN's eight, another counts SICA's eight, and a third counts the seven that sit on the isthmus. Each is correct for its own definition, but only the seven-country list matches the geography most people mean.

## Which Central American countries are easiest to visit together?

Because the countries are small and the region is narrow, many travelers string several together. Some common combinations:

- **Guatemala, Belize and Honduras:** Maya ruins and Caribbean islands near the border region.
- **Nicaragua, Costa Rica and Panama:** the southern run, from volcanoes to beaches to the canal.
- **El Salvador and Guatemala:** a short hop that works for a long weekend or a surf trip.

Border rules, transport and entry requirements change, so check your government's travel advice before you go. ## FAQ

### How many countries are in Central America?

Seven: Belize, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama. The UN's statistical grouping lists eight because it adds Mexico.

### Is Mexico in Central America?

Usually no. Mexico is generally counted as part of North America, though the UN's M49 geographic scheme places it in Central America for statistical purposes.

### Is the Dominican Republic in Central America?

No. It is a Caribbean island nation, although it has been a full member of the Central American Integration System since 2013.

### Is Belize part of Central America?

Yes. Belize is considered part of both Central America and the Caribbean, and it is the only Central American country where English is the official language.

### Are the countries in Central America all sovereign?

Yes. All seven are independent, sovereign countries, so each one counts toward a world total of 195.
