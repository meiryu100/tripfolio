The best **travel map app** is the one that lets you mark countries on a world map, fix mistakes, counts your total on a list you can name, and keeps your data private, all for free or for a price you understand. Most of them look alike in a screenshot. The differences are in the counting, the regions, the sharing and what happens to your data, so those are the things to check before you spend an evening entering your trips.

This guide gives you eight questions to ask of any travel map app, explains why each one matters, and shows how to test an app in five minutes. It stays on criteria rather than ranking brands, because app features and prices change often.

## What does a travel map app actually do?

A travel map app is a tracker for where you've been and where you want to go. You tap or search for places, they get colored in, and the app shows some statistics: how many countries, what percentage of the world, sometimes which continents.

Beyond that core, apps split into a few types:

- **Country checklists.** Simple: mark countries, see a count. Quick to set up and often enough.
- **Scratch-map style apps.** Same idea with a poster look. If you're weighing a physical poster against a digital map, our [scratch map alternative guide](/blog/scratch-map-alternative) compares them.
- **Globe and pin apps.** A 3D globe or a map with pins for cities, airports or national parks.
- **Social travel maps.** You also see where friends have been, follow people and compare maps.
- **Travel journals with a map.** The map is the index to your trips, photos and notes.

Knowing which type you want saves time. A person who only wants the number "47 countries" has different needs from someone who wants a record of every trip.

## Question 1: How does it count countries?

This is the most important question and the one apps rarely explain. There is no single worldwide list of countries. The UN has [193 member states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states) and [two non-member observer states](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/non-member-states), the Holy See and the State of Palestine. Together that is 195, which is the list many travelers use. Other lists include more places, such as territories, or disputed regions.

So two apps can show different totals for the same trips. Look for an app that:

- says which list it counts against, such as "x / 195";
- separates sovereign countries from territories, so Puerto Rico or Hong Kong don't quietly inflate your number;
- lets you decide the rule for what counts as a visit, since layovers and cruise stops are personal calls. Our guide to [what counts as visiting a country](/blog/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country) covers the common rules, and [how many countries there are in the world](/blog/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world) explains the lists.

## Question 2: Can you undo a mistake?

Tapping the wrong country is easy, especially on a phone. A good app lets you tap again to remove it, without a confirmation maze. If an app makes removal hard, or treats your list as permanent, it will frustrate you the first time you reconsider a layover.

## Question 3: Does it cover regions inside countries?

If you road trip, a map that only shows "USA" as one color hides most of your travel. Check whether the app breaks big countries into states or provinces. Many apps do this for the United States only. Few cover Canada or Australia.

On Tripfolio, the USA is split into 50 states with an "x / 50 states" counter, and Canada and Australia are split into their provinces, territories and states in the same way. Details are in the guides to the [US states visited map](/blog/us-states-visited-map) and the [Canada provinces visited map](/blog/canada-provinces-visited-map).

## Question 4: Is there a wishlist?

Half the pleasure of a map is the blank space. A second color for "want to visit" turns the map from a record into a plan. It also gives you a quick answer to "where should we go next?" when you look at it with a partner or friends.

Ask whether the wishlist is part of the app or an extra you pay for, and whether it can show you which friends have already been to a place you want to see.

## Question 5: What does it cost, really?

Many travel map apps are free to download and then ask for money inside the app. The common patterns:

- **Fully free.** Fine for most people if the basics work.
- **Free with limits.** You can mark countries, but things like extra map styles, more regions or exports sit behind a paywall.
- **Subscription.** Check the renewal period and how to cancel before you start.
- **One-time purchase.** Simple, but make sure it carries over if you change phones.

Before you enter your list, check the store page for what is free and what isn't. It's annoying to find a paywall after you have entered 60 countries by hand.

## Question 6: Where does it work?

Store apps usually target one platform, so check yours is supported before you commit. A web-based map opens in the browser on a phone or a laptop with nothing to install, and your map follows you across devices. The trade-off is that a web page needs a connection, so check whether offline use matters to you.

Tripfolio is web only today: free, English, light and dark mode, and it works on a phone. There is no app in the app stores yet. If you want a home-screen icon, you can add the site to your home screen from the browser.

## Question 7: What happens to your data and your privacy?

A map of everywhere you've been is personal. It says when your home is empty and what you like. Before signing up, look for:

- **A clear privacy setting.** Can you make your account private, or hide parts of it?
- **What the store says.** On the Apple App Store, [privacy labels](https://www.apple.com/privacy/labels/) sort an app's data into "Data Linked to You", "Data Not Linked to You" and "Data Not Collected", and list the purposes. They are a fast way to compare apps before you install them.
- **How photos are handled.** If an app imports photos to find your countries, find out whether it reads them on your device or uploads them.
- **A way to delete your account and data.** It should be easy to find, not buried.

On Tripfolio you can make an account private and hide visited countries, the wishlist, trips and photos separately. When you use Import from photos, the countries are read from the photo location data on your device. Photos without location data are skipped.

## Question 8: Can you share it without friction?

A map is more fun when someone sees it. Good sharing means a card or an image with your count on it, and a link that opens for a friend without them having to install anything or create an account.

Tripfolio makes a share card of your map with "X countries · Y% of the world", and the link opens a public page. You can build the card from the try-it map without signing up.

## How do you test a travel map app in five minutes?

Run the same quick test on any app you're considering:

1. **Mark five countries, including one tiny one.** Try [Singapore](/countries/singapore), [Luxembourg](/countries/luxembourg) or a Caribbean island. Is it easy to hit on a phone?
2. **Remove one.** How many taps does it take?
3. **Look at the number.** Does it say what it's counting against? Does a territory change it?
4. **Open the settings.** Find privacy and account deletion. If you can't find them in two minutes, that tells you something.
5. **Share the result.** Does the friend you send it to need to install anything?

If an app passes all five, you can safely invest the time to enter your whole list.

## What should you add first?

Whatever app you choose, a good first session goes like this: list the countries you remember region by region, then scroll through your photo albums for the ones you forgot, then add five places for the wishlist. When you reach 10, 25, 50 or 100 countries, those are the usual milestone numbers travelers celebrate, and an app that tracks them gives you something to aim at.

## FAQ

### What is the best app to track countries visited?

The best one is the one that matches how you count: it names the list it uses, lets you undo, covers the regions you travel in and respects your privacy. Run the five-minute test above on two or three and keep the one that feels right.

### Is there a free travel map app?

Yes. Several are free, and Tripfolio is a free web-based one with a visited and wishlist map, a country counter against the list of 195 countries, and regional maps for the USA, Canada and Australia. Check any app's store page for what sits behind a paywall.

### How many countries should a travel map app show?

Most use 195, which is the 193 UN member states plus the two observer states. Some also color territories. Prefer an app that shows territories on the map but keeps them out of the total.

### Can a travel map app find my countries from my photos?

Some can. Photos often carry location data, so an app can read it and suggest countries. Check whether the reading happens on your device and whether photos without location data are skipped. Tripfolio's Import from photos does this on the device.

### Do I need to install an app to keep a travel map?

Not necessarily. A web-based travel map works in the browser on any phone or computer. You only need a store app if you want features the web version doesn't offer.
